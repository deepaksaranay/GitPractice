# Engineering Mechanics

## 1.1 Basic Concepts
Engineering mechanics studies bodies at rest (statics) and in motion (dynamics). Dynamics has two parts: kinematics (motion without force) and kinetics (motion with force).
- Scalar: magnitude only (mass, time, work, energy, power, speed). Vector: magnitude + direction (force, displacement, velocity, acceleration, momentum, moment).
- Rigid body: the distance between any two particles stays constant. All statics in SSC JE assumes rigid bodies.
- Principle of transmissibility: a force may be moved anywhere along its line of action without changing its external effect on a rigid body. It applies only to rigid bodies and only to external effects.
- Particle: a body with mass but negligible dimensions, so rotation is ignored.
- Units: 1 N = 1 kg·m/s²; 1 kgf = 9.81 N; 1 dyne = 10⁻⁵ N; 1 J = 10⁷ erg; 1 hp (metric) = 735.5 W; 1 hp (British) = 746 W.
PYQ-TREND: Transmissibility applies to rigid bodies only. Definition of a newton or a dyne and their conversion. Scalar vs vector identification (work and energy are scalar; moment is vector; pressure is scalar).

## 1.2 Systems of Forces
| System | Description | Example |
| Coplanar concurrent | All forces in one plane meeting at a point | Forces on a ring with strings |
| Coplanar parallel (like/unlike) | Lines of action parallel | Loads on a beam |
| Coplanar non-concurrent non-parallel | General planar | Ladder problem |
| Collinear | Same line of action | Tug of war |
| Non-coplanar concurrent | 3D forces at a point | Tripod legs |

### Resultant of Two Forces (Parallelogram Law)
F: R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cosθ)
F: tan α = Q sinθ / (P + Q cosθ)   (α = angle of R with P)
F: θ = 0° → R = P+Q (maximum);  θ = 180° → R = |P−Q| (minimum);  θ = 90° → R = √(P²+Q²)
F: Two equal forces P at angle θ: R = 2P cos(θ/2)
- Triangle law: if two forces are represented by two sides of a triangle taken in order, the closing side taken in opposite order is the resultant.
- Polygon law: extension for more than two forces.
- Resolution: Fx = F cosθ, Fy = F sinθ. ΣFx and ΣFy give R = √(ΣFx² + ΣFy²), tanα = ΣFy/ΣFx.
PYQ-TREND: Two equal forces at 120° give R equal to each force. Two equal forces at 60° give R = √3 P. The resultant of two forces is maximum when they are collinear and like. "Magnitude of resultant of 3 N and 4 N at right angles = 5 N" is a typical conceptual check.

## 1.3 Equilibrium
- Conditions in a plane: ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0, ΣM = 0.
- Two-force member: forces must be equal, opposite and collinear.
- Three-force member: forces must be concurrent (or parallel) and coplanar.
- Lami's theorem: if three coplanar concurrent forces are in equilibrium, each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two.
F: P/sin α = Q/sin β = R/sin γ   (α = angle between Q and R, etc.)
- Free body diagram (FBD): isolate the body and show all external forces and reactions. Roller support gives 1 reaction (normal), hinge/pin gives 2 (H, V), fixed support gives 3 (H, V, M).
- Varignon's theorem (principle of moments): the moment of the resultant about any point equals the algebraic sum of the moments of the components about the same point.
- Couple: two equal, opposite, parallel non-collinear forces. Moment of couple = F × arm. It is the same about every point in the plane (free vector). A couple cannot be balanced by a single force, only by an equal and opposite couple.
- A force can be replaced by an equal parallel force at another point plus a couple (force–couple system).
PYQ-TREND: Lami's theorem is valid only for three concurrent forces. A couple's moment is independent of the reference point. Number of reactions at fixed, hinged and roller supports. Varignon's theorem relates to moments.
TIP: When three forces act on a body and two meet at a point, the third must pass through the same point. This shortcut solves many ladder and hinge problems in seconds.

## 1.4 Friction
Friction is the tangential resistance at the contact of two surfaces. It always opposes the relative motion or the tendency of motion.
- Static friction is self-adjusting up to the limiting value. Limiting friction F = μN.
- Dynamic (kinetic) friction is slightly less than limiting friction. Rolling friction < sliding friction.
- Laws of dry (Coulomb) friction: F ∝ N; independent of apparent contact area; depends on nature of surfaces; kinetic friction roughly independent of velocity.
F: μ = F/N = tan φ     (φ = angle of friction)
F: Angle of repose α = φ   (max inclination at which a body rests on its own)
F: Body on incline (no applied force), tendency to slide down: equilibrium if tanθ ≤ μ
F: Pull P parallel to plane to move up: P = W(sinθ + μcosθ);  to move down: P = W(μcosθ − sinθ)
F: Least force to drag a body on a horizontal plane: P_min = W sinφ, applied at angle φ to the horizontal
- Cone of friction: the cone generated by the resultant reaction with semi-vertex angle φ.
- Ladder problems: take friction at floor and wall, ΣFx=ΣFy=ΣM=0. If wall is smooth, only floor friction acts.
- Belt friction (see TOM): T₁/T₂ = e^(μθ).
- Screw jack: efficiency η = tanα / tan(α+φ); max efficiency = (1−sinφ)/(1+sinφ); self-locking if φ > α, and then η < 50%.
PYQ-TREND: Angle of repose = angle of friction. Efficiency of a self-locking screw is less than 50%. Force of friction is independent of the area of contact. The least force to drag a body on a rough horizontal plane is W sin φ. Coefficient of friction depends on the nature of surfaces.
WARN: μ can exceed 1 (e.g., rubber on dry concrete, clean metals in vacuum). Don't reject an answer just because μ > 1.

## 1.5 Centroid and Centre of Gravity
- Centre of gravity (CG): point through which the total weight acts. Centroid: geometric centre of an area/line/volume. They coincide for uniform density in a uniform gravity field.
- An axis of symmetry always contains the centroid.
| Shape | Centroid location |
| Rectangle b×h | h/2 from base |
| Triangle | h/3 from base (2h/3 from apex) |
| Semicircle (area), radius r | 4r/(3π) from diameter |
| Quarter circle | 4r/(3π) from each straight edge |
| Semicircular arc (wire) | 2r/π from diameter |
| Right circular solid cone | h/4 from base |
| Hollow cone (surface) | h/3 from base |
| Solid hemisphere | 3r/8 from flat face |
| Hollow hemisphere (thin shell) | r/2 from flat face |
| Parabolic spandrel (y = kx²), base a, height h | x̄ = 3a/4, ȳ = 3h/10 |
F: x̄ = Σ(Aᵢxᵢ)/ΣAᵢ ;  ȳ = Σ(Aᵢyᵢ)/ΣAᵢ   (subtract cut-out areas)
- Pappus–Guldinus: Area of surface of revolution = length of curve × distance travelled by its centroid. Volume of revolution = area × distance travelled by its centroid (2πȳ).
PYQ-TREND: CG of a solid cone at h/4 from base, solid hemisphere at 3r/8, semicircle at 4r/3π, triangle at h/3. These four come almost every year.

## 1.6 Moment of Inertia (Area)
Second moment of area I = ∫y² dA. It measures resistance to bending (beam stiffness).
F: Parallel axis theorem: I_AB = I_G + A h²
F: Perpendicular axis theorem (plane lamina): I_zz = I_xx + I_yy  (polar MOI J)
F: Radius of gyration k = √(I/A)
| Section | I about centroidal axis |
| Rectangle b × d (about axis ∥ b) | bd³/12 |
| Rectangle about base | bd³/3 |
| Triangle (about centroidal axis ∥ base) | bh³/36 |
| Triangle about base | bh³/12 |
| Triangle about apex (∥ base) | bh³/4 |
| Circle dia d | πd⁴/64 (polar πd⁴/32) |
| Hollow circle | π(D⁴−d⁴)/64 |
| Semicircle about diameter | πr⁴/8 = πd⁴/128 |
| Semicircle about centroidal axis ∥ diameter | 0.11 r⁴ |
| Square side a about diagonal | a⁴/12 (same as about centroidal axis) |
- Mass MOI (dynamics): thin rod about centre mL²/12, about end mL²/3; solid disc mR²/2; ring mR²; solid sphere 2/5 mR²; hollow sphere 2/3 mR².
PYQ-TREND: I of triangle about base bh³/12 vs about centroid bh³/36. MOI of a square about its diagonal equals that about the centroidal axis. Polar MOI of circle πd⁴/32. Mass MOI of solid sphere 2mR²/5.
TIP: For "about base" values, use I_base = I_G + A(h/2)² for a rectangle: bh³/12 + bh·h²/4 = bh³/3.

## 1.7 Kinematics of Particles
F: v = u + at;  s = ut + ½at²;  v² = u² + 2as;  s_nth = u + a(2n−1)/2
- Free fall: a = g = 9.81 m/s². Time of flight for vertical projection = 2u/g; max height = u²/2g.
F: Projectile: T = 2u sinθ/g;  H = u² sin²θ/(2g);  R = u² sin2θ/g;  R_max = u²/g at θ = 45°
F: Complementary angles (θ, 90°−θ) give equal range. Path is parabola: y = x tanθ − gx²/(2u²cos²θ)
- Angular motion: ω = ω₀ + αt; θ = ω₀t + ½αt²; ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ. v = rω, a_t = rα, a_n = v²/r = rω².
- Relative velocity: v_AB = v_A − v_B.
PYQ-TREND: Maximum range at 45°; at maximum range H = R/4. Equal range for 30° and 60°. Time to reach max height = u sinθ/g. Centripetal acceleration ω²r directed towards centre.

## 1.8 Kinetics – Newton's Laws, Work, Energy, Power
- First law: inertia. Second law: F = dp/dt = ma. Third law: action and reaction are equal and opposite and act on different bodies.
- D'Alembert's principle converts a dynamics problem into an equivalent statics problem by adding the inertia force (−ma).
- Lift problems: going up with acceleration a → R = m(g + a); going down → R = m(g − a); free fall → R = 0.
- Work W = F·s cosθ (scalar, joule). Power P = W/t = F·v. Energy: KE = ½mv², PE = mgh; rotational KE = ½Iω².
- Work-energy principle: net work done = change in KE.
- Conservation of energy holds if only conservative forces (gravity, spring) do work.
F: Spring energy = ½kx²; springs in series 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂; in parallel k = k₁ + k₂
F: Rolling body KE = ½mv²(1 + k²/r²); acceleration down incline a = g sinθ/(1 + k²/r²)
- Rolling race down an incline (who reaches first): solid sphere (k²/r² = 2/5) > solid cylinder (1/2) > hollow sphere (2/3) > ring (1).
PYQ-TREND: D'Alembert principle: inertia force. Apparent weight in a lift. Rolling-without-slipping order. Unit of power and conversion of hp. Spring constants in series/parallel.

## 1.9 Impulse, Momentum and Collisions
F: Impulse = F·Δt = Δ(mv);  Linear momentum p = mv
F: Coefficient of restitution e = (v₂ − v₁)/(u₁ − u₂) = velocity of separation / velocity of approach
- Perfectly elastic: e = 1, KE conserved. Perfectly plastic (inelastic): e = 0, bodies move together, max KE loss. 0 < e < 1 for real collisions.
- Momentum is conserved in all collisions (no external impulse). KE is conserved only for e = 1.
- Ball dropped from height h, bounce height h₁ = e²h; e = √(h₁/h).
F: Loss of KE in direct impact = m₁m₂(u₁−u₂)²(1−e²) / [2(m₁+m₂)]
- Angular momentum L = Iω; conserved if no external torque (ice skater pulls in arms → ω increases).
- Recoil of gun: m_b v_b = m_g v_g.
PYQ-TREND: Value of e for perfectly elastic and plastic impact. Equal masses with elastic head-on collision exchange velocities. Bounce height h₁ = e²h. Momentum conserved in all impacts.

## 1.10 Simple Machines
- Mechanical advantage MA = W/P (load/effort). Velocity ratio VR = distance moved by effort / distance moved by load. Efficiency η = MA/VR.
- Ideal machine: η = 100%, MA = VR. Law of machine: P = mW + C (linear). Max MA = 1/m; max efficiency = 1/(m·VR).
- Reversible machine: η > 50%. Self-locking (irreversible) machine: η ≤ 50% – the load does not run back when effort is removed.
| Machine | Velocity Ratio |
| Simple wheel & axle | D/d |
| Differential wheel & axle | 2D/(d₁ − d₂) |
| Weston differential pulley | 2D/(D − d) |
| First system of pulleys (n movable pulleys) | 2ⁿ |
| Second system (n pulleys total) | n |
| Third system | 2ⁿ − 1 |
| Simple screw jack | 2πL/p |
| Differential screw jack | 2πL/(p₁ − p₂) |
| Worm & worm wheel | (L·T)/(r·n) – depends on starts n |
| Single purchase crab winch | (L/r)(T₂/T₁) |
PYQ-TREND: Condition for reversibility (η > 50%) is asked almost every year. Velocity ratio of the first/second/third system of pulleys and of the differential pulley block.
Q: Explain the difference between a reversible and self-locking machine, and prove that a machine is self-locking when its efficiency is less than 50%.
A machine is reversible if the load moves back when effort is removed. Let effort P lift load W through distance y, effort moving x. Input = Px, output = Wy, friction loss = Px − Wy. On reversal, the load W does work Wy, which must overcome the same friction loss Px − Wy. Reversal needs Wy > Px − Wy, i.e., Wy/Px > 1/2, i.e., η > 50%. So a machine with η ≤ 50% is self-locking.
