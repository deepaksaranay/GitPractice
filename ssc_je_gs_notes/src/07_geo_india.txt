# Indian Geography

## 7.1 India – Location and Size
- Latitudes 8°4'N to 37°6'N; longitudes 68°7'E to 97°25'E. Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) passes through 8 states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram.
- Area 3.287 million km² – 7th largest country (2.4% of world area); population most populous in the world (overtook China in 2023, UN estimate). N–S extent ≈ 3,214 km; E–W ≈ 2,933 km. Land frontier ≈ 15,200 km; coastline ≈ 7,516.6 km (mainland ≈ 6,100 km).
- Standard Meridian 82°30'E passes through Mirzapur (UP); IST = GMT + 5:30. Time difference between Arunachal and Gujarat ≈ 2 hours.
- Southernmost point: Indira Point (Great Nicobar; earlier Pygmalion Point); mainland southernmost – Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin). Northernmost: Indira Col (Siachen). Easternmost: Kibithu (Arunachal). Westernmost: Guhar Moti (Gujarat).
- Neighbours by length of border: Bangladesh (longest, ≈ 4,096 km), China, Pakistan, Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan, Afghanistan (≈ 106 km, shortest). States touching Bangladesh: West Bengal (longest), Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram. States bordering Nepal: Uttarakhand, UP, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim. States bordering China: Ladakh (UT), HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal.
- Boundary lines: Radcliffe (India–Pakistan, India–Bangladesh), McMahon (India–China in the east, 1914 Simla), Durand (Pakistan–Afghanistan, 1893). Palk Strait separates India and Sri Lanka (Adam's Bridge/Ram Setu, Gulf of Mannar). Ten Degree Channel separates Andaman and Nicobar; Eight Degree Channel separates Minicoy and Maldives; Nine Degree Channel separates Lakshadweep (Kavaratti) and Minicoy.
- Largest state by area: Rajasthan; smallest: Goa. Most populous: Uttar Pradesh. Largest UT: Ladakh. Smallest UT: Lakshadweep. Longest coastline state: Gujarat (≈ 1,600 km); Andhra Pradesh is second.
F: Tropic of Cancer – 8 states | Standard meridian 82°30'E (Mirzapur) | Longest border – Bangladesh | Largest state – Rajasthan | Smallest – Goa | Longest coastline – Gujarat
PYQ-TREND: States crossed by Tropic of Cancer. Standard meridian city. Neighbour with longest/shortest border. Ten Degree Channel. McMahon/Durand/Radcliffe lines. Southernmost point Indira Point. Palk Strait.

## 7.2 Physiographic Divisions
- The Himalayas: Trans-Himalaya (Karakoram, Ladakh, Zaskar); Greater Himalaya/Himadri (highest peaks, avg 6,000 m; Kangchenjunga 8,586 m – highest peak in India; K2/Godwin Austen 8,611 m in PoK claimed territory), Lesser Himalaya/Himachal (Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mahabharat; hill stations Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, Darjeeling), Outer Himalaya/Shiwaliks (youngest; duns like Dehradun; Kosi-Teesta). Purvanchal (Patkai, Naga, Mizo/Lushai hills).
- Important passes: Karakoram (Ladakh–China, highest motorable route region), Zoji La (Srinagar–Leh; Zojila tunnel), Banihal (Jawahar tunnel, Jammu–Srinagar), Khardung La (Ladakh; among the highest motorable passes), Rohtang (HP – Manali–Lahaul; Atal Tunnel 2020), Shipki La (HP–Tibet; Sutlej enters India), Lipulekh (Uttarakhand; Kailash Mansarovar route), Mana (Uttarakhand), Nathu La (Sikkim–Tibet; trade route reopened 2006), Jelep La (Sikkim–Tibet), Bomdi La (Arunachal), Diphu (Arunachal–Myanmar), Bara-lacha La (HP; Manali–Leh), Palakkad Gap (Kerala–TN, Western Ghats), Thal Ghat & Bhor Ghat (Western Ghats, Maharashtra).
- Northern Plains: Bhabar (pebbles, streams disappear), Terai (marshy), Bhangar (old alluvium; kankar), Khadar (new alluvium; fertile, floodplains).
- Peninsular Plateau: Central Highlands (Malwa, Bundelkhand, Chota Nagpur – 'mineral heartland'), Deccan Plateau. Aravallis – oldest fold mountains; Guru Shikhar (Mount Abu) highest. Vindhyas and Satpuras (Dhupgarh highest peak of Satpura in Pachmarhi). Western Ghats (Sahyadri – continuous; Anamudi 2,695 m – highest peak of South India, in Kerala; Doddabetta – Nilgiris), Eastern Ghats (discontinuous; Mahendragiri; Jindhagada/Arma Konda highest). Nilgiris – meeting point of Western and Eastern Ghats.
- Indian Desert (Thar): mostly in Rajasthan; Luni the only significant river.
- Coastal plains: West – Konkan (Mumbai–Goa), Kanara, Malabar (Kerala; backwaters/kayals, Vembanad lake). East – Northern Circars (Odisha–AP), Coromandel (TN). Chilika lake (Odisha – largest brackish lagoon in India), Pulicat (AP–TN), Kolleru (freshwater, AP).
- Islands: Andaman & Nicobar (572 islands; Barren Island – only active volcano of India; Saddle Peak highest; capital Sri Vijaya Puram, earlier Port Blair) and Lakshadweep (coral; 36 islands; capital Kavaratti).
F: Highest peak in India – Kangchenjunga | South India – Anamudi | Aravalli – oldest fold mountains (Guru Shikhar) | Barren Island – only active volcano | Chilika – largest brackish lagoon

## 7.4 Climate
- Tropical monsoon climate. Seasons (IMD): winter (Dec–Feb), summer (Mar–May), south-west monsoon (Jun–Sep), retreating/north-east monsoon (Oct–Nov).
- South-west monsoon onset over Kerala ≈ 1 June; covers India by ≈ 8 July (earlier 15 July). Two branches: Arabian Sea branch and Bay of Bengal branch.
- Mawsynram (Meghalaya) – highest average rainfall in the world (Cherrapunji/Sohra nearby). Least rainfall – Leh, Jaisalmer region.
- Western disturbances bring winter rain to north-west India (good for rabi wheat). Tamil Nadu (Coromandel) gets most rain in winter from the north-east (retreating) monsoon.
- Local winds: Loo (hot dry, north India, summer), Mango showers (Kerala & Karnataka – pre-monsoon, help mango ripening), Cherry blossoms/Coffee showers (Karnataka), Nor'westers / Kalbaisakhi (Bengal–Assam; 'tea showers' in Assam), Bardoli Chheerha (Assam).
- El Niño weakens the Indian monsoon; La Niña strengthens it. Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) positive phase helps the monsoon.
F: Monsoon onset Kerala ~1 June | Highest rainfall – Mawsynram | Kalbaisakhi – Bengal | Mango showers – Kerala/Karnataka | NE monsoon – Tamil Nadu winter rain
PYQ-TREND: Local winds (Loo, Mango showers, Kalbaisakhi). Mawsynram highest rainfall. Tamil Nadu's winter rainfall from NE monsoon. Western disturbances and rabi crops. El Niño effect.

## 7.5 Soils and Natural Vegetation
| Soil | Region | Features |
| Alluvial | Northern plains, deltas | Most widespread (≈ 43%); rich in potash, poor in nitrogen; khadar & bhangar |
| Black (regur) | Deccan trap – Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP | Best for cotton; high moisture retention; rich in iron, lime, magnesia; self-ploughing (cracks) |
| Red & yellow | Eastern & southern Deccan, Odisha, Chhattisgarh | Red due to iron oxide (ferric); yellow when hydrated |
| Laterite | High rainfall: Western Ghats, Kerala, Karnataka, Odisha hills | Leaching; good for tea, coffee, cashew; used as bricks |
| Arid (desert) | Rajasthan | Sandy, saline; low humus |
| Saline & alkaline (usar, reh, kallar) | Dry areas, Punjab–Haryana–UP | Treated with gypsum |
| Peaty / marshy | Kerala (Kuttanad), Sundarbans | High organic matter |
| Forest / mountain | Himalayas | Acidic at heights |
- ICAR classified 8 major soil groups. Soil erosion control: contour ploughing, terrace farming, shelter belts, strip cropping.
- Forest cover (India State of Forest Report 2021): forest cover ≈ 21.7% of geographical area; forest + tree cover ≈ 24.6%. Largest forest cover by area – Madhya Pradesh; by percentage – Mizoram.
- Forest types: tropical evergreen (Western Ghats, NE; >200 cm rain; ebony, mahogany, rosewood), tropical deciduous/monsoon (most widespread; teak, sal, sandalwood), thorn (Rajasthan; babul, khejri), montane, mangrove/tidal (Sundarbans – sundari tree; largest mangrove forest; Bhitarkanika in Odisha).
F: Black soil – cotton | Laterite – leaching, tea/coffee/cashew | Alluvial – most widespread | Largest forest area – MP | Highest % forest – Mizoram
PYQ-TREND: Black soil and cotton (regur). Laterite soil formed by leaching. Red colour due to iron oxide. Gypsum for alkaline soil. Sundarbans mangroves and sundari tree. State with largest forest cover (MP).
