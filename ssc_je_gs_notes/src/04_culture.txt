# Art & Culture

## 4.1 Classical Dances (Sangeet Natak Akademi recognises 8)
| Dance | State | Key facts |
| Bharatanatyam | Tamil Nadu | Oldest; based on Natyashastra; earlier 'Sadir/Dasiattam'; revived by Rukmini Devi Arundale (Kalakshetra) |
| Kathakali | Kerala | Dance-drama; elaborate make-up (green = noble 'pacha'); male performers traditionally; Ramayana–Mahabharata themes; Kerala Kalamandalam founded by Vallathol |
| Mohiniyattam | Kerala | 'Dance of the enchantress'; solo female; lasya style; white-gold costume |
| Kuchipudi | Andhra Pradesh | Named after village; Siddhendra Yogi; dancing on brass plate (Tarangam) |
| Odissi | Odisha | Tribhanga posture; Mahari tradition (temple dancers) and Gotipua (boys); Kelucharan Mohapatra |
| Kathak | Uttar Pradesh (north India) | Only classical dance of the north; Mughal influence; chakkars; Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras gharanas; Birju Maharaj |
| Manipuri | Manipur | Ras Lila themes; Vaishnavism; Jagoi; no ghungroo; Pung cholom (drum dance) |
| Sattriya | Assam | Introduced by Shankardev (15th century) in sattras; recognised in 2000 |
- The Ministry of Culture also lists Chhau (Odisha/Jharkhand/West Bengal – masked martial dance; UNESCO ICH 2010) among classical forms; Sangeet Natak Akademi lists 8.
- Natyashastra by Bharat Muni is the source text of Indian dance, drama and music. Nine rasas (navarasa).
F: Bharatanatyam – TN | Kathakali & Mohiniyattam – Kerala | Kuchipudi – AP | Odissi – Odisha | Kathak – UP | Manipuri – Manipur | Sattriya – Assam
PYQ-TREND: Classical dance–state matching is one of the most frequent culture questions. Sattriya's founder (Shankardev). Natyashastra author. Kathak as the only northern classical form. Famous dancers and their forms (Birju Maharaj – Kathak, Kelucharan Mohapatra – Odissi, Yamini Krishnamurthy – Bharatanatyam/Kuchipudi, Sonal Mansingh – Odissi).
TIP: Two from Kerala ("K & M": Kathakali, Mohiniyattam); Kuchipudi is a village in Krishna district (AP).

## 4.3 Music
- Two systems: Hindustani (north; Persian influence; ragas linked to time of day) and Carnatic (south; kriti-based; trinity – Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri; 72 melakarta ragas; Purandaradasa 'father of Carnatic music').
- Hindustani forms: Dhrupad (oldest, Dagar family), Khayal (Amir Khusrau / popularised by Sadarang), Thumri (Lucknow; light classical), Tappa (Punjab camel riders), Dadra, Ghazal, Qawwali (Amir Khusrau).

## 4.5 Architecture and Monuments
- Temple styles: Nagara (north – curvilinear shikhara; e.g., Khajuraho, Sun Temple Konark), Dravida (south – pyramidal vimana, gopuram gateways; e.g., Brihadeshwara, Meenakshi), Vesara (mixed – Chalukya & Hoysala; e.g., Hoysaleswara at Halebidu, Chennakesava at Belur). Odisha subtype: Deul (Lingaraja, Jagannath, Konark).
- Konark Sun Temple: Narasimhadeva I (Eastern Ganga, 13th century); chariot with 24 wheels and 7 horses; 'Black Pagoda'. Jagannath temple Puri: 'White Pagoda' (Anantavarman Chodaganga).
- Khajuraho (MP): Chandelas; Kandariya Mahadeva temple. Dilwara temples (Mount Abu): Jain, marble (Vimal Shah and Tejpal). Sanchi Stupa: Ashoka (MP). Ajanta (paintings; Buddhist; Maharashtra) and Ellora (Buddhist, Hindu, Jain; 34 caves). Elephanta (Trimurti Shiva; Mumbai). Meenakshi temple, Madurai (Nayakas). Virupaksha, Hampi. Somnath (Gujarat; first of 12 jyotirlingas).
- Indo-Islamic: Qutub Minar (red sandstone, 5 storeys), Alai Darwaza (first true arch & dome), Tughlaqabad, Humayun's Tomb (first garden tomb; Hamida Banu Begum; architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyas; precursor of Taj), Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza – tallest gateway; Panch Mahal; Jodha Bai's palace), Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Gol Gumbaz (Bijapur – whispering gallery; Muhammad Adil Shah), Charminar (Hyderabad, 1591), Char Minar's founder Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah.
- Colonial: Victoria Memorial (Kolkata; William Emerson), Gateway of India (Mumbai, 1924; George Wittet; for George V's 1911 visit), India Gate (Delhi; Edwin Lutyens; All-India War Memorial), Rashtrapati Bhavan (Lutyens), Parliament House (old – Lutyens & Herbert Baker; circular), Howrah Bridge (cantilever, 1943), Writers' Building (Kolkata).
- Others: Hawa Mahal (Jaipur; Sawai Pratap Singh; architect Lal Chand Ustad), Jantar Mantar (Sawai Jai Singh II – five observatories: Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Varanasi, Mathura), Golden Temple (Amritsar), Sun Temple Modhera (Gujarat; Solanki), Rani ki Vav (Patan, Gujarat; stepwell; UNESCO; featured on ₹100 note), Kirti Stambh & Vijay Stambh (Chittorgarh; Vijay Stambh built by Rana Kumbha after victory over Mahmud Khilji), Statue of Unity (Kevadia, Gujarat – Sardar Patel, 182 m, tallest statue).

## 4.7 Literature, Languages and Calendars
- 22 languages in the Eighth Schedule (originally 14). Added by: 21st Amendment 1967 – Sindhi; 71st Amendment 1992 – Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali; 92nd Amendment 2003 – Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali.
- Classical languages: Tamil (first, 2004), Sanskrit (2005), Telugu & Kannada (2008), Malayalam (2013), Odia (2014); in October 2024 Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese and Bengali were added → 11 in total.
- Sahitya Akademi (1954) – literature, 24 languages; Sangeet Natak Akademi (1952) – performing arts; Lalit Kala Akademi (1954) – fine arts; National School of Drama (1959).
- Scripts: Brahmi (parent of most Indian scripts), Kharosthi (right to left), Gurmukhi (Guru Angad), Ol Chiki (Santhali – Raghunath Murmu), Meetei Mayek (Manipuri).
F: 22 scheduled languages | Classical: Tamil 2004 first; 11 in total after 2024 additions | Sahitya Akademi 1954
