# General Science – Biology

## 12.1 Cell – The Unit of Life
- Cell discovered by Robert Hooke (1665, cork – dead cells; book Micrographia). Living cells first seen by Anton van Leeuwenhoek (father of microbiology). Nucleus discovered by Robert Brown (1831). Cell theory: Schleiden (plants) & Schwann (animals), 1838–39; Rudolf Virchow – 'omnis cellula e cellula' (cells arise from pre-existing cells).
- Prokaryotes (no true nucleus/membrane-bound organelles; bacteria, cyanobacteria) vs eukaryotes (true nucleus). Largest cell: ostrich egg; smallest: Mycoplasma (PPLO); longest human cell: neuron; largest human cell: ovum; smallest human cell: sperm (by volume); RBCs lack nuclei in mammals.
| Organelle | Function / fact |
| Cell membrane (plasma membrane) | Selectively permeable; fluid mosaic model (Singer & Nicolson) |
| Cell wall | Plants (cellulose), fungi (chitin), bacteria (peptidoglycan); absent in animal cells |
| Nucleus | Control centre; contains DNA/chromosomes; nucleolus makes ribosomes |
| Mitochondria | 'Powerhouse of the cell'; ATP via aerobic respiration; own DNA & ribosomes (semi-autonomous); maternal inheritance |
| Chloroplast | Photosynthesis; chlorophyll (Mg at centre); own DNA; only in plants & algae |
| Ribosome | Protein synthesis; non-membranous; 70S (prokaryotes) / 80S (eukaryotes) |
| Endoplasmic reticulum | Rough (protein synthesis, with ribosomes), smooth (lipid synthesis, detoxification) |
| Golgi apparatus | Packaging & secretion ('post office'); forms lysosomes, acrosome of sperm; Camillo Golgi |
| Lysosome | 'Suicide bags' – digestive enzymes (Christian de Duve) |
| Vacuole | Storage; large central vacuole in plant cells (tonoplast) |
| Centrosome | Cell division (spindle) in animal cells |
| Plastids | Chloroplast (green), chromoplast (colour – tomato, carrot), leucoplast (storage – starch) |
- Cell division: mitosis (somatic cells; two identical diploid daughter cells; growth & repair) and meiosis (germ cells; four haploid gametes; reduction division; crossing over → variation).
- DNA: double helix (Watson & Crick, 1953, with Rosalind Franklin's X-ray data); bases A–T, G–C; sugar deoxyribose. RNA: single strand, uracil instead of thymine, ribose sugar. Gene – unit of heredity (term by Johannsen). Human chromosomes: 46 (23 pairs; 22 autosomes + XX/XY).
F: Cell – Robert Hooke 1665 | Nucleus – Robert Brown | Powerhouse – mitochondria | Suicide bag – lysosome | Protein factory – ribosome | DNA double helix – Watson & Crick 1953 | 46 chromosomes
PYQ-TREND: Discoverer of cell and nucleus. Powerhouse (mitochondria), suicide bags (lysosomes), protein synthesis (ribosomes). Organelles with own DNA. Cell wall material. Mitosis vs meiosis. Watson & Crick. Number of human chromosomes. Largest/longest human cell.

## 12.2 Classification of Living Beings
- Carl Linnaeus – father of taxonomy; binomial nomenclature (genus + species, e.g., Homo sapiens, Panthera tigris, Mangifera indica). Hierarchy: Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species.
- Whittaker's five kingdoms (1969): Monera (bacteria, cyanobacteria – prokaryotes), Protista (unicellular eukaryotes – Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena, Plasmodium), Fungi (heterotrophs; chitin walls; yeast, mushroom, Penicillium), Plantae, Animalia. (Carl Woese's three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya.)
- Viruses: acellular, crystallisable, living only inside host cells (link between living and non-living); DNA or RNA; first discovered – Tobacco Mosaic Virus (Ivanovsky 1892; named by Beijerinck). Viroids (RNA only), prions (proteins – mad cow disease).
- Plant kingdom: thallophyta (algae), bryophyta ('amphibians of the plant kingdom' – mosses, liverworts), pteridophyta (ferns – first vascular plants), gymnosperms (naked seeds – pine, cycas, deodar; Sequoia – tallest tree), angiosperms (flowering; seeds in fruits; monocots – one cotyledon, parallel veins, fibrous roots – rice, wheat, maize, banana, coconut, grasses; dicots – two cotyledons, reticulate veins, tap roots – pea, mango, neem).
- Animal kingdom: Porifera (sponges, pores), Coelenterata/Cnidaria (Hydra, jellyfish, corals), Platyhelminthes (flatworms – tapeworm, liver fluke), Nematoda (roundworms – Ascaris, filarial worm), Annelida (segmented – earthworm, leech; earthworm 'farmer's friend'), Arthropoda (largest phylum – insects, spiders, crabs, prawns; jointed legs; open circulation), Mollusca (snail, octopus, Pila; octopus/squid – 'devil fish'), Echinodermata (starfish, sea urchin – all marine; water vascular system), Chordata (notochord).
- Vertebrate classes: Pisces (gills; 2-chambered heart; cold-blooded), Amphibia (frog, toad, salamander; 3-chambered heart; live on land and water), Reptilia (lizards, snakes, crocodiles – 4-chambered heart in crocodiles; cold-blooded), Aves (birds; warm-blooded; hollow bones; 4-chambered heart), Mammalia (mammary glands, hair, external ears; 4-chambered heart; warm-blooded; egg-laying mammals – platypus, echidna).
- Odd facts: whale, dolphin, bat are mammals; seahorse, shark are fish; starfish, jellyfish, silverfish, cuttlefish are not fish; penguins are birds; kiwi, ostrich, emu – flightless birds; ostrich lays the largest egg; hummingbird – smallest bird; blue whale – largest animal.
F: Father of taxonomy – Linnaeus | Five kingdoms – Whittaker 1969 | TMV – first virus | Largest phylum – Arthropoda | Bryophytes – amphibians of plant kingdom | Platypus – egg-laying mammal
PYQ-TREND: Binomial nomenclature and Linnaeus. Five-kingdom classification. Scientific names of common organisms (Homo sapiens, Panthera leo/tigris, Mangifera indica, Oryza sativa, Triticum aestivum, Azadirachta indica – neem, Ficus benghalensis – banyan). Viruses as a link. Animals wrongly called fish. Heart chambers by class. Bryophytes/pteridophytes characteristics.

## 12.4 Human Body Systems
### Digestive system
- Mouth (saliva – salivary amylase/ptyalin digests starch), oesophagus, stomach (HCl kills germs & activates pepsin; pepsin – proteins; renin – milk protein in infants), small intestine (longest part ≈ 6–7 m; duodenum receives bile & pancreatic juice; villi absorb nutrients), large intestine (absorbs water; caecum & vestigial appendix), rectum, anus.
- Liver: largest gland (≈ 1.5 kg); secretes bile (stored in gall bladder; emulsifies fats; no enzymes); stores glycogen; detoxification; makes urea. Pancreas: both exocrine (pancreatic juice – trypsin, lipase, amylase) and endocrine (islets of Langerhans – insulin (β cells, lowers sugar), glucagon (α cells, raises sugar)).
- Digestion end products: carbohydrates → glucose; proteins → amino acids; fats → fatty acids & glycerol.
### Respiratory system
- Nose → pharynx → larynx (voice box) → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli (gas exchange). Diaphragm helps breathing. Normal breathing rate ≈ 12–18 per minute (adult). Tidal volume ≈ 500 mL; vital capacity ≈ 3.5–4.5 L.
- Aerobic respiration: glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + energy (38 ATP theoretically); anaerobic in muscles → lactic acid (fatigue/cramps); in yeast → ethanol + CO₂ (fermentation). Glycolysis in cytoplasm; Krebs cycle in mitochondria.
### Circulatory system
- Discovered by William Harvey (1628). Heart: 4 chambers (2 atria, 2 ventricles); left ventricle thickest wall (pumps to whole body); pacemaker – SA node; heartbeat ≈ 72/min; normal BP 120/80 mm Hg (measured by sphygmomanometer). Arteries carry blood away from heart (oxygenated except pulmonary artery); veins carry towards heart (deoxygenated except pulmonary vein). Aorta – largest artery; vena cava – largest vein. Capillaries – exchange. First heart transplant – Christiaan Barnard (South Africa, 1967).
- Blood (≈ 5–6 litres in adults; ≈ 7% of body weight; pH 7.4): plasma (≈ 55%; 90% water) and cells: RBCs (erythrocytes – haemoglobin carries O₂; no nucleus in mammals; lifespan ≈ 120 days; made in red bone marrow; destroyed in spleen – 'graveyard of RBCs'), WBCs (leucocytes – immunity; neutrophils most abundant, lymphocytes make antibodies), platelets (thrombocytes – clotting; fall in dengue). Clotting needs vitamin K, calcium, fibrinogen → fibrin. Haemophilia – bleeder's disease (X-linked). Anticoagulant: heparin.
- Blood groups (Karl Landsteiner, 1900): A, B, AB, O. Universal donor O (negative), universal recipient AB (positive). Rh factor (Landsteiner & Wiener, Rhesus monkey); Rh incompatibility – erythroblastosis fetalis. ABO is an example of multiple alleles and codominance.
- Lymphatic system: lymph nodes, spleen, thymus (T-cell maturation), tonsils.
### Excretory system
- Kidneys (≈ 1 million nephrons each – functional unit; Bowman's capsule, glomerulus). Urine: water, urea, uric acid, creatinine, salts; yellow colour from urochrome. Dialysis for kidney failure. Kidney stones – calcium oxalate. Other excretory organs: skin (sweat), lungs (CO₂), liver (urea formation – ornithine cycle).
### Nervous system
- Brain (≈ 1.35 kg; protected by skull & meninges; cerebrospinal fluid): cerebrum (largest; intelligence, memory, thinking, voluntary actions), cerebellum (balance, posture, coordination – affected by alcohol), medulla oblongata (involuntary actions – heartbeat, breathing, vomiting, sneezing), hypothalamus (body temperature, hunger, thirst, controls pituitary), thalamus (relay). Spinal cord – reflex actions. Neuron – structural & functional unit; synapse – junction. 12 pairs of cranial nerves, 31 pairs of spinal nerves.
### Endocrine system (hormones)
| Gland | Hormone | Function / disorder |
| Pituitary ('master gland'; at base of brain) | Growth hormone, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin, ADH (vasopressin), oxytocin | GH excess – gigantism/acromegaly; deficiency – dwarfism; ADH deficiency – diabetes insipidus |
| Thyroid (neck; largest endocrine gland) | Thyroxine (needs iodine), calcitonin | Goitre, cretinism (children), myxoedema (adults), hyperthyroidism – Graves' disease |
| Parathyroid | Parathormone | Blood calcium (raises) |
| Adrenal (on kidneys) | Adrenaline ('fight or flight', emergency hormone), cortisol, aldosterone | Addison's disease (cortex deficiency) |
| Pancreas (islets) | Insulin, glucagon | Diabetes mellitus (insulin deficiency) |
| Thymus | Thymosin | Immunity (T cells); shrinks in adults |
| Pineal | Melatonin | Sleep–wake cycle (biological clock) |
| Testes / ovaries | Testosterone / oestrogen, progesterone | Secondary sexual characters; pregnancy |
### Skeletal and muscular system
- Adult human: 206 bones (newborn ≈ 270–300). Largest/longest bone – femur (thigh); smallest – stapes (in middle ear); strongest – femur; hardest substance – tooth enamel. Skull 22 bones; vertebral column 33 vertebrae (26 in adults after fusion); ribs 12 pairs (floating ribs – last 2 pairs). Joints: ball-and-socket (shoulder, hip), hinge (knee, elbow), pivot (neck – atlas & axis), gliding (wrist). Largest muscle – gluteus maximus; smallest – stapedius.
- Sense organs: eye (cornea – transplanted; iris gives colour; retina – rods & cones; blind spot – no receptors; yellow spot – sharpest vision), ear (hearing & balance; ossicles – malleus, incus, stapes; cochlea – hearing; semicircular canals – balance), tongue, nose, skin (largest organ).
- Teeth: 32 in adults (dental formula 2123), 20 milk teeth. Enamel hardest substance in body.
F: Largest gland – liver | Master gland – pituitary | Largest organ – skin | 206 bones; femur longest; stapes smallest | RBC life 120 days | Universal donor O, recipient AB | BP 120/80 | Insulin – β cells | Graveyard of RBCs – spleen
PYQ-TREND: Largest gland, master gland, largest organ. Blood groups – universal donor/recipient and Landsteiner. Hormone–gland–disorder matching (insulin – diabetes, thyroxine – goitre, ADH – diabetes insipidus, adrenaline – emergency). Function of cerebellum and medulla. Number of bones; smallest and largest bones. RBC lifespan, site of formation and destruction. Bile has no enzymes. HCl in stomach. Heart chambers and pacemaker. Nephron as kidney unit. Haemophilia.

## 12.6 Plant Biology
- Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (light, chlorophyll). Oxygen released comes from water (photolysis). Light reaction in grana (thylakoids), dark reaction (Calvin cycle) in stroma. Red and blue light most effective; green least (reflected). C₃ plants (wheat, rice) vs C₄ plants (maize, sugarcane – more efficient). CAM plants (cactus, pineapple – stomata open at night).
- Transpiration: loss of water vapour through stomata (guard cells); cools the plant, aids water ascent. Xylem conducts water & minerals (dead tissue mostly – vessels, tracheids); phloem conducts food (sieve tubes, companion cells; living). Annual rings in trees (dendrochronology – estimating age).
- Plant hormones: auxins (growth, apical dominance, phototropism; IAA; rooting of cuttings), gibberellins (stem elongation, bolting, seedless fruits; first found in rice 'foolish seedling' disease), cytokinins (cell division; delay ageing), abscisic acid ('stress hormone'; dormancy; stomatal closure), ethylene (only gaseous hormone; fruit ripening).
- Tropisms: phototropism (light – shoots +ve), geotropism (gravity – roots +ve), hydrotropism (water), thigmotropism (touch – tendrils). Nastic movements: Mimosa pudica (touch-me-not) folds leaves – seismonasty.
- Reproduction: vegetative propagation – potato (stem tuber), ginger (rhizome), onion (bulb), sweet potato & dahlia (root tuber), Bryophyllum (leaf buds), grafting (mango, rose), layering, tissue culture (micropropagation). Pollination by wind (anemophily – grasses, maize), insects (entomophily), water (hydrophily – Vallisneria), birds (ornithophily). Double fertilisation unique to angiosperms (Nawaschin).
- Edible parts: potato – stem; carrot, radish, beetroot, sweet potato – root; onion, garlic – bulb (leaves/stem); ginger, turmeric – rhizome (stem); cauliflower – inflorescence; cabbage, lettuce, spinach – leaves; saffron – stigma; clove – flower bud; black pepper – fruit; coconut water – liquid endosperm; apple – thalamus (false fruit); mango – mesocarp; banana – parthenocarpic fruit.
- Nitrogen fixation: Rhizobium in root nodules of legumes (peas, beans, groundnut); Azotobacter (free-living); cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc – paddy fields); Azolla (fern with Anabaena) – biofertiliser.
- Economic plants: rubber (Hevea brasiliensis), quinine (Cinchona bark – malaria), morphine (opium poppy), cocaine (coca), tea (Camellia sinensis), coffee (Coffea arabica), neem (Azadirachta indica – natural pesticide), Bt cotton (Bacillus thuringiensis gene – pest resistant; India's only approved GM crop for cultivation), golden rice (vitamin A).
F: O₂ in photosynthesis from water | Ethylene – ripening (gaseous) | ABA – stress | Gibberellin – stem elongation | Potato – stem | Ginger – rhizome | Saffron – stigma | Clove – flower bud | Rhizobium – legumes

## 12.7 Genetics, Evolution, Ecology
- Gregor Mendel – father of genetics; experiments on garden pea (Pisum sativum), 7 traits; laws of dominance, segregation, independent assortment. Monohybrid ratio 3:1 (phenotype), 1:2:1 (genotype); dihybrid 9:3:3:1. Rediscovered in 1900 (de Vries, Correns, Tschermak). T.H. Morgan – fruit fly (Drosophila), linkage. Sex of child determined by father's chromosome (X or Y). Human Genome Project completed in 2003.
- Biotechnology: recombinant DNA (Cohen & Boyer); first cloned mammal – Dolly the sheep (1996, Ian Wilmut); DNA fingerprinting (Alec Jeffreys; in India – Lalji Singh, 'father of Indian DNA fingerprinting'); CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing (Nobel 2020 – Charpentier & Doudna); first transgenic animal; insulin by genetically engineered bacteria; stem cells; Flavr Savr tomato (first GM food).
- Evolution: Lamarck (inheritance of acquired characters; 'use and disuse'), Darwin (natural selection; 'Origin of Species' 1859; 'survival of the fittest' – phrase by Herbert Spencer), Hugo de Vries (mutation theory). Homologous organs (same origin, different function – forelimbs of man, bat, whale; divergent evolution) vs analogous organs (different origin, same function – wings of bird & insect; convergent evolution). Vestigial organs: appendix, wisdom teeth, coccyx, nictitating membrane. Archaeopteryx – connecting link between reptiles and birds.
- Ecology: ecosystem (biotic + abiotic; term by A.G. Tansley), food chain (producers → herbivores → carnivores → decomposers); 10% law of energy transfer (Lindeman); pyramid of energy always upright; pyramid of numbers inverted in a tree ecosystem; biomass pyramid inverted in the ocean. Biomagnification (DDT, mercury increase along the food chain). Keystone species. Biodiversity hotspots in India: Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, Sundaland (Nicobar).
- IUCN Red List: critically endangered, endangered, vulnerable. Great Indian Bustard – critically endangered (state bird of Rajasthan). Ex-situ (zoos, seed banks, botanical gardens) vs in-situ (national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves) conservation. Environment Protection Act 1986; Wildlife Protection Act 1972; Forest Conservation Act 1980; Biological Diversity Act 2002. Chipko movement (1973, Uttarakhand – Sunderlal Bahuguna, Gaura Devi; earlier Bishnoi sacrifice – Amrita Devi, 1730); Appiko movement (Karnataka); Narmada Bachao Andolan (Medha Patkar); Silent Valley movement (Kerala).
F: Father of genetics – Mendel (pea) | 3:1 and 9:3:3:1 | Dolly – 1996 | Origin of Species – Darwin 1859 | 10% law – Lindeman | Ecosystem – Tansley | Chipko – 1973
PYQ-TREND: Mendel's ratios and pea plant. Darwin vs Lamarck. Homologous vs analogous organs. Vestigial organs. 10% law of energy transfer. Biomagnification. Biodiversity hotspots of India. Chipko and Bishnoi movements. Dolly the sheep. DNA fingerprinting in India (Lalji Singh). Sex determination by father.
