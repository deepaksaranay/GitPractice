# Medieval Indian History

## 2.1 Arab and Turkish Invasions
- Muhammad bin Qasim conquered Sindh in 712 CE, defeating Raja Dahir – the first Arab conquest.
- Mahmud of Ghazni raided India 17 times (1000–1027 CE); plundered Somnath temple (1025). Court scholar Al-Biruni wrote Kitab-ul-Hind (Tahqiq-i-Hind); poet Firdausi wrote Shahnama.
- Muhammad Ghori: First Battle of Tarain (1191) – Prithviraj Chauhan won; Second Battle of Tarain (1192) – Ghori won, laying the foundation of Turkish rule. Battle of Chandawar (1194) – defeated Jaichand of Kannauj. Prithviraj's court poet Chand Bardai wrote Prithviraj Raso.
F: 712 – Sindh (Qasim) | 1025 – Somnath raid (Mahmud) | 1191/1192 – Tarain I/II | 1194 – Chandawar
PYQ-TREND: Battles of Tarain and their winners. Al-Biruni's Kitab-ul-Hind. Muhammad bin Qasim and Sindh. Prithviraj Raso by Chand Bardai.

## 2.2 Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)
| Dynasty | Period | Founder |
| Slave (Mamluk/Ilbari) | 1206–1290 | Qutbuddin Aibak |
| Khilji | 1290–1320 | Jalaluddin Khilji |
| Tughlaq | 1320–1414 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq |
| Sayyid | 1414–1451 | Khizr Khan |
| Lodi (first Afghan dynasty) | 1451–1526 | Bahlul Lodi |
### Slave dynasty
- Qutbuddin Aibak: 'Lakh Bakhsh' (giver of lakhs); started Qutub Minar (completed by Iltutmish; repaired by Firoz Tughlaq); built Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque (Delhi) and Adhai-din-ka-Jhonpra (Ajmer); died falling from a horse playing chaugan (polo) at Lahore.
- Iltutmish: real founder/consolidator; capital Delhi; introduced silver tanka and copper jital; Iqta system; 'Turkan-i-Chahalgani' (Group of Forty); obtained investiture from the Abbasid Caliph.
- Razia Sultan (1236–40): first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate.
- Balban: theory of kingship (divine right, 'Zil-i-Ilahi' – shadow of God), Sijda and Paibos customs, crushed the Forty, Diwan-i-Arz (military department).
### Khilji dynasty
- Alauddin Khilji: market control (price regulation), Dagh (branding horses) and Huliya/Chehra (descriptive rolls of soldiers), standing army paid in cash, Alai Darwaza, Siri fort, Hauz-i-Alai; general Malik Kafur conquered the Deccan; Amir Khusrau ('Parrot of India', invented sitar/tabla per tradition, Khazain-ul-Futuh) at his court; Chittor siege 1303 (Padmavati legend by Jayasi's Padmavat, 1540).
### Tughlaq dynasty
- Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq built Tughlaqabad; Muhammad bin Tughlaq: transferred capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri) in 1327, token currency of copper/brass, Khurasan and Qarachil expeditions, taxation in the Doab; Ibn Battuta (Moroccan traveller) visited and wrote Rehla; called 'mixture of opposites' and 'wise fool'.
- Firoz Shah Tughlaq: imposed jizya on Brahmins, built canals, towns (Firozabad, Hisar, Jaunpur), brought two Ashokan pillars (Topra and Meerut) to Delhi, Diwan-i-Khairat (charity) and Diwan-i-Bandagan (slaves). Timur invaded Delhi in 1398 (during Nasiruddin Mahmud).
### Lodi dynasty
- Sikandar Lodi founded Agra (1504), introduced Gaz-i-Sikandari (measuring yard), wrote poetry as 'Gulrukhi'. Ibrahim Lodi lost the First Battle of Panipat (1526) to Babur.
F: Qutub Minar – Aibak began, Iltutmish completed | Market reforms – Alauddin | Capital to Daulatabad, token currency – Muhammad bin Tughlaq | Agra founded – Sikandar Lodi (1504)
PYQ-TREND: Muhammad bin Tughlaq's experiments (capital shift, token currency). Alauddin's market control and Dagh system. Iltutmish's tanka and jital. Razia only woman ruler. Ibn Battuta. Amir Khusrau. Founder of Agra. Balban's theory of kingship (Sijda, Paibos).
WARN: Agra was founded by Sikandar Lodi, not by Akbar. Akbar built the Agra Fort; Fatehpur Sikri was his new capital.

## 2.3 Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdoms
- Vijayanagara founded in 1336 by Harihara I and Bukka I (Sangama dynasty) with guidance of Vidyaranya; capital Hampi (on the Tungabhadra; UNESCO site). Dynasties: Sangama, Saluva, Tuluva, Aravidu.
- Krishnadeva Raya (Tuluva, 1509–29): greatest ruler; wrote Amuktamalyada (Telugu); court had Ashtadiggajas (eight poets) including Tenali Ramakrishna and Allasani Peddana ('Andhra Kavita Pitamaha'); built Vitthala and Hazara Rama temples. Portuguese travellers Domingo Paes and Fernao Nuniz described the city; also Abdur Razzaq (Persian) and Nicolo Conti (Italian, Devaraya I).
- Battle of Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi), 1565: Deccan Sultanates defeated Rama Raya; Hampi destroyed.
- Bahmani kingdom: founded 1347 by Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (Hasan Gangu); capital Gulbarga, later Bidar; minister Mahmud Gawan (madrasa at Bidar). Split into five Deccan Sultanates: Bijapur (Adil Shahi – Gol Gumbaz), Golconda (Qutb Shahi – Charminar built by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, founder of Hyderabad), Ahmednagar (Nizam Shahi), Berar (Imad Shahi), Bidar (Barid Shahi).
F: Vijayanagara 1336 (Harihara & Bukka) | Talikota 1565 | Bahmani 1347 (Hasan Gangu) | Gol Gumbaz – Bijapur | Charminar – Golconda/Hyderabad
PYQ-TREND: Founders of Vijayanagara and Bahmani. Krishnadeva Raya's Amuktamalyada and Ashtadiggajas. Battle of Talikota 1565. Gol Gumbaz location. Foreign travellers to Vijayanagara.

## 2.6 Marathas and Sikhs
- Shivaji (1630–80), born at Shivneri fort; coronation at Raigad 1674 ('Chhatrapati'); Ashtapradhan council (Peshwa – PM, Amatya – finance, Senapati, etc.); revenue taxes Chauth (1/4) and Sardeshmukhi (1/10 extra); killed Afzal Khan (1659); Treaty of Purandar (1665) with Jai Singh; escaped from Agra (1666).
- Peshwas: Balaji Vishwanath (first powerful Peshwa), Baji Rao I (expansion north – 'Hindu Pad Padshahi'), Balaji Baji Rao (Nana Saheb; Panipat III loss), Madhav Rao I, Baji Rao II (Treaty of Bassein 1802 – subsidiary alliance; last Peshwa).
- Anglo-Maratha wars: First (1775–82; Treaty of Salbai), Second (1803–05), Third (1817–18; end of Peshwaship).
- Sikh Gurus: Nanak (1st), Angad (Gurmukhi script), Amar Das (Manji system, langar strengthened), Ram Das (founded Amritsar; land from Akbar), Arjan Dev (compiled Adi Granth 1604, built Harmandir Sahib; executed by Jahangir), Hargobind (Akal Takht; militarisation), Har Rai, Har Krishan (youngest), Tegh Bahadur (executed by Aurangzeb 1675), Gobind Singh (10th; Khalsa founded 1699 at Anandpur Sahib; last human guru; Guru Granth Sahib declared eternal Guru).
- Banda Bahadur – first Sikh political leader after Gobind Singh. Maharaja Ranjit Singh – Sikh empire, capital Lahore; Treaty of Amritsar (1809) with the British; Koh-i-noor. Anglo-Sikh wars 1845–46 and 1848–49 → Punjab annexed by Dalhousie (1849).
F: Shivaji coronation 1674 Raigad | Khalsa 1699 (Guru Gobind Singh) | Adi Granth 1604 (Guru Arjan Dev) | Amritsar founded – Guru Ram Das
PYQ-TREND: Shivaji's Ashtapradhan and Chauth–Sardeshmukhi. Founder of Khalsa and year. Guru who compiled Adi Granth; Guru who founded Amritsar; Guru who introduced Gurmukhi. Treaty of Bassein. Maharaja Ranjit Singh's capital.
