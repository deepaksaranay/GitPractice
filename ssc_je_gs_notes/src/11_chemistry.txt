# General Science – Chemistry

## 11.1 Matter and Atomic Structure
- States: solid, liquid, gas, plasma (fourth state – Sun, lightning, neon signs), Bose–Einstein condensate (fifth; S.N. Bose & Einstein; near absolute zero).
- Sublimation (solid → gas directly): camphor, naphthalene (mothballs), iodine, dry ice (solid CO₂), ammonium chloride. Deposition is the reverse.
- Pure substances: elements and compounds. Mixtures: homogeneous (solutions – air, brass, salt water) and heterogeneous (sand + water, milk is colloidal). Colloids show Tyndall effect: milk, fog, smoke, blood, butter, jelly, ink. Emulsion: milk, butter (liquid in liquid). Aerosol: fog, mist (liquid in gas), smoke (solid in gas). Foam: shaving cream. Gel: jelly, cheese.
- Separation: distillation (liquids with different boiling points; fractional distillation for petroleum and liquid air), chromatography (dyes, pigments), centrifugation (cream from milk, blood tests), sublimation, crystallisation (salt from sea water; purification of alum/copper sulphate), evaporation, filtration, decantation, separating funnel (oil and water), magnetic separation.
- Atomic number (Z) = number of protons; mass number (A) = protons + neutrons. Isotopes: same Z, different A (protium, deuterium, tritium; C-12/C-14; U-235/U-238). Isobars: same A, different Z (Ar-40/Ca-40). Isotones: same number of neutrons. Isoelectronic species: same number of electrons.
- Dalton's atomic theory (1808). Avogadro number 6.022 × 10²³ per mole. Molar volume of gas at STP 22.4 L.
- Valency and electronic configuration (K, L, M, N shells hold 2, 8, 18, 32 = 2n²).

## 11.2 Periodic Table
- Dobereiner's triads, Newlands' law of octaves, Mendeleev's periodic table (atomic mass; left gaps for gallium & germanium – eka-aluminium, eka-silicon), Moseley's modern periodic law (atomic number). Modern table: 7 periods, 18 groups; 118 elements (Oganesson, 118, heaviest).
- Groups: 1 alkali metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr – very reactive, stored in kerosene), 2 alkaline earth metals (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra), 13 boron family, 14 carbon family, 15 nitrogen family (pnictogens), 16 oxygen family (chalcogens), 17 halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At), 18 noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn; inert; complete octet).
- Trends: across a period – atomic size decreases, electronegativity and ionisation energy increase; down a group – size increases, electronegativity decreases, metallic character increases.
- Extremes: most electronegative – fluorine; most reactive non-metal – fluorine; most reactive metal – caesium/francium; lightest element – hydrogen; lightest metal – lithium; heaviest naturally occurring – uranium; densest – osmium; most abundant in universe – hydrogen; in Earth's crust – oxygen (metal – aluminium); in atmosphere – nitrogen; in human body – oxygen (by mass; carbon second); hardest natural substance – diamond; liquid metal – mercury (also caesium, gallium, francium near room temperature); liquid non-metal – bromine; best conductor – silver; poorest conductor metal – lead/bismuth; highest melting metal – tungsten; lowest melting metal – mercury; metalloids – B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te.
- Noble gas uses: helium – balloons, airships, deep-sea diving (with oxygen; avoids bends), MRI cooling; neon – advertising signs (red glow); argon – bulbs, welding; krypton & xenon – flash lamps, headlights; radon – radiotherapy (radioactive).

## 11.3 Chemical Reactions, Acids, Bases and Salts
- Reaction types: combination, decomposition (thermal, electrolytic, photolytic – silver chloride darkens in light, used in photography), displacement (more reactive metal displaces less reactive; iron nail in copper sulphate turns brown), double displacement (precipitation), redox (oxidation = loss of electrons/gain of oxygen; reduction = gain of electrons/loss of oxygen). Exothermic (respiration, burning, neutralisation, quicklime + water) and endothermic (photosynthesis, dissolving ammonium chloride/nitrate).
- Corrosion (rusting of iron needs oxygen and water: Fe₂O₃·xH₂O); prevented by galvanisation (zinc coating), painting, oiling, alloying (stainless steel), electroplating, cathodic protection. Rancidity of fats (oxidation) prevented by antioxidants, nitrogen flushing in chip packets, refrigeration.
- Catalysts: speed up reactions without being consumed – iron (Haber process for ammonia), V₂O₅ (Contact process for H₂SO₄), nickel (hydrogenation of oils to vanaspati ghee), platinum (Ostwald process for nitric acid; catalytic converters), enzymes (biocatalysts).
- Acids: sour, turn blue litmus red, pH < 7, release H⁺. Bases: bitter, soapy, turn red litmus blue, pH > 7. Indicators: litmus (from lichens), phenolphthalein (colourless in acid, pink in base), methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in base), turmeric (red in base).
- pH scale (Sørensen): 0–14; pure water 7; human blood ≈ 7.4 (slightly basic); gastric juice ≈ 1.5–2 (HCl); lemon juice ≈ 2.2; vinegar ≈ 3; milk ≈ 6.5; tomato ≈ 4.2; saliva ≈ 6.5–7.5; sea water ≈ 8; milk of magnesia ≈ 10; acid rain pH < 5.6; tooth decay begins below pH 5.5.

## 11.4 Metals, Non-metals and Alloys
- Metals: lustrous, malleable (gold most), ductile (gold, silver), good conductors (silver > copper > gold > aluminium), sonorous. Exceptions: mercury liquid; sodium & potassium soft (cut with knife) and stored in kerosene; gallium & caesium melt in the hand; iodine (non-metal) is lustrous; graphite (non-metal) conducts electricity; diamond (non-metal) hardest.
- Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au > Pt.
- Extraction: ore → concentration (froth flotation for sulphide ores) → roasting (sulphide ores in air) / calcination (carbonate ores without air) → reduction (carbon; electrolysis for highly reactive metals like Na, Al – Hall–Héroult) → refining (electrolytic). Thermite reaction (Al + Fe₂O₃) – welding rail tracks.
- Ores: iron – haematite (Fe₂O₃), magnetite (Fe₃O₄ – richest, magnetic), siderite; aluminium – bauxite; copper – chalcopyrite, cuprite; zinc – zinc blende, calamine; lead – galena; mercury – cinnabar; tin – cassiterite; sodium – rock salt; magnesium – magnesite, dolomite, carnallite; calcium – limestone; uranium – pitchblende; thorium – monazite.
- Iron forms: pig iron/cast iron (≈ 2–4% C, brittle), wrought iron (purest, < 0.25% C), steel (0.1–1.5% C). Stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni; chromium prevents rust).

## 11.5 Carbon Compounds and Everyday Chemistry
- Carbon forms millions of compounds due to catenation and tetravalency. Hydrocarbons: alkanes (single bonds, CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ – methane, ethane, propane, butane), alkenes (double bond – ethene ripens fruits), alkynes (triple – ethyne/acetylene for welding & artificial ripening with calcium carbide – banned).
- Fuels: LPG – mainly butane + propane (ethyl mercaptan added for smell); CNG – mainly methane; biogas – methane (≈ 55–75%) + CO₂; coal gas – H₂ + CH₄ + CO; water gas – CO + H₂; producer gas – CO + N₂; natural gas – methane. Calorific value highest – hydrogen. Octane number (petrol anti-knock quality), cetane number (diesel). Ethanol blending in petrol (E20 target).
- Alcohols: ethanol (in beverages, spirit; 'rectified spirit' 95%; 'absolute alcohol' 100%; denatured with methanol – poisonous, causes blindness). Fermentation of sugar by yeast produces ethanol and CO₂.
- Soaps (sodium/potassium salts of fatty acids; saponification – fat + NaOH) don't lather well in hard water (Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ form scum). Detergents work in hard water. Temporary hardness (bicarbonates) removed by boiling or Clark's method (lime); permanent hardness (chlorides, sulphates) by washing soda or ion exchange (permutit/zeolite).
- Polymers: natural (rubber – isoprene, cellulose, starch, proteins, silk, wool), synthetic: polythene (packaging), PVC (pipes), Teflon/PTFE (non-stick; most chemically resistant), Bakelite (thermosetting – switches, handles), nylon (first synthetic fibre – parachutes, ropes; Wallace Carothers), polyester/terylene/dacron (clothes; PET bottles), acrylic (artificial wool), rayon (artificial silk from cellulose), melamine (crockery; thermoset), neoprene (synthetic rubber), Kevlar (bulletproof vests). Thermoplastics soften on heating (polythene, PVC); thermosets don't (Bakelite, melamine).
- Glass: silica (sand) + soda + lime. Types: soda-lime (windows), Pyrex/borosilicate (heat resistant, lab ware), flint/lead crystal (lenses, prisms), photochromatic (sunglasses – silver halide), coloured glass – cobalt oxide (blue), chromium oxide (green), manganese dioxide (violet), ferrous oxide (green), cuprous oxide (red), gold chloride (ruby red).
- Cement: Portland cement (Joseph Aspdin, 1824) – limestone + clay; gypsum added to slow setting. Concrete = cement + sand + gravel + water.
- Fertilisers: urea (46% N – highest), ammonium sulphate, DAP, NPK. Pesticides: DDT (banned in agriculture; biomagnification), BHC.
- Environmental chemistry: greenhouse gases – CO₂, methane, N₂O, CFCs, water vapour; CFCs deplete ozone (Montreal Protocol); acid rain – SO₂ and NOx (damaged Taj Mahal – 'marble cancer'); photochemical smog (ozone, PAN); Bhopal gas tragedy (1984) – methyl isocyanate (MIC) from Union Carbide; Minamata disease – mercury; Itai-itai – cadmium; blue baby syndrome – nitrates in water; fluorosis – excess fluoride; arsenic poisoning – groundwater in Bengal; carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin (≈ 200× stronger than O₂) – poisonous.
- Fire extinguishers: CO₂ (electrical fires), soda-acid, foam (oil fires – don't use water on oil/electrical fires).
- Food chemistry: artificial sweeteners (saccharin, aspartame – unstable when heated, sucralose); preservatives (sodium benzoate, salt, sugar, vinegar); MSG (Ajinomoto, flavour enhancer); baking powder = baking soda + tartaric acid.
- Medicines: analgesics (aspirin, paracetamol), antipyretics (paracetamol – fever), antibiotics (penicillin – Alexander Fleming, 1928), antiseptics (dettol, savlon, tincture iodine – on living tissue) vs disinfectants (phenol – on non-living objects), tranquilisers, antacids, antihistamines.
