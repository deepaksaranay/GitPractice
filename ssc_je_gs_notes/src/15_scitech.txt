# Science & Technology, Space and Computers

## 15.1 Indian Space Programme
- INCOSPAR set up in 1962 (Vikram Sarabhai – 'father of the Indian space programme'); ISRO formed 15 August 1969; Department of Space 1972. First rocket launched from Thumba (TERLS, Kerala) on 21 November 1963 (Nike-Apache). Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota – main launch site (SHAR). A second spaceport is being built at Kulasekarapattinam (Tamil Nadu) for SSLV.
- Launch vehicles: SLV-3 (1980, Rohini – first Indian launch from Indian soil; project director A.P.J. Abdul Kalam), ASLV, PSLV ('workhorse'; polar & sun-synchronous orbits; record 104 satellites in one launch, Feb 2017; launched Chandrayaan-1 and Mangalyaan), GSLV (geosynchronous; cryogenic upper stage), GSLV Mk III / LVM3 ('Bahubali'; heaviest; launched Chandrayaan-2 & 3; OneWeb satellites), SSLV (small satellites, 2022–23). Agnibaan & Vikram-S – private rockets (Skyroot's Vikram-S – first private launch, Nov 2022).

- NISAR – NASA–ISRO SAR satellite (2025). Shukrayaan (Venus orbiter) planned. Chandrayaan-4 (sample return) planned.
- Satellites types: geostationary (≈ 36,000 km, 24 h – communication, weather: INSAT/GSAT), polar/sun-synchronous (≈ 500–900 km – remote sensing, mapping: IRS, Cartosat, Oceansat, RISAT).
- ISRO chairmen: Vikram Sarabhai (first), M.G.K. Menon, Satish Dhawan, U.R. Rao, K. Kasturirangan, G. Madhavan Nair, K. Radhakrishnan, A.S. Kiran Kumar, K. Sivan, S. Somanath, V. Narayanan (from January 2025).
- World space firsts: Sputnik-1 (USSR, 4 Oct 1957 – first artificial satellite); Laika (first animal in orbit, 1957); Yuri Gagarin (first human in space, 12 April 1961, Vostok-1); Valentina Tereshkova (first woman, 1963); Alexei Leonov (first spacewalk, 1965); Neil Armstrong & Buzz Aldrin (Moon, Apollo 11, 1969); Hubble Space Telescope (1990); James Webb Space Telescope (2021, at L2). International Space Station (1998 onwards). Sunita Williams – Indian-origin astronaut with long stays on ISS.

## 15.2 Defence & Nuclear Science
- Indian atomic programme: Homi J. Bhabha ('father of Indian nuclear programme'; TIFR 1945; Atomic Energy Commission 1948; BARC). Three-stage nuclear programme: PHWR (natural uranium) → fast breeder reactors (plutonium; Kalpakkam PFBR) → thorium-based reactors (India has large thorium reserves in monazite sands of Kerala).
- Pokhran-I (18 May 1974, 'Smiling Buddha'); Pokhran-II (11 & 13 May 1998, 'Operation Shakti'; Kalam & R. Chidambaram). India's nuclear doctrine: No First Use, credible minimum deterrence. Nuclear triad completed with INS Arihant (2018).
- Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP, 1983) led by A.P.J. Abdul Kalam ('Missile Man of India').
- Anti-satellite missile test – Mission Shakti (27 March 2019); India fourth country with ASAT capability.
F: Bhabha – nuclear programme | Pokhran-I 1974, Pokhran-II 1998 | Mission Shakti ASAT 2019 | Three-stage programme ends with thorium
PYQ-TREND: Father of Indian nuclear programme. Pokhran test code names and years. Thorium-based third stage. Mission Shakti (ASAT). Kalam and IGMDP.

## 15.3 Computer Fundamentals
- Charles Babbage – 'father of the computer' (Difference Engine, Analytical Engine); Ada Lovelace – first programmer. Alan Turing – father of theoretical computer science/AI (Turing machine). John von Neumann – stored-program architecture.
| Generation | Technology | Example |
| First (1940–56) | Vacuum tubes | ENIAC (first general-purpose electronic computer, 1946), UNIVAC |
| Second (1956–63) | Transistors | IBM 1401 |
| Third (1964–71) | Integrated circuits | IBM 360 |
| Fourth (1971–present) | Microprocessors (VLSI) | Intel 4004 (first microprocessor, 1971), PCs |
| Fifth (present & beyond) | AI, ULSI, parallel processing, quantum | – |
- India's first computer: TIFRAC (1960, TIFR, Mumbai); first computer installed – HEC-2M at ISI Kolkata (1956). India's first supercomputer: PARAM 8000 (1991, C-DAC Pune; Vijay Bhatkar – father of Indian supercomputers). National Supercomputing Mission; AIRAWAT (AI supercomputer).
- Types: supercomputer (fastest; weather forecasting), mainframe (large organisations, banks), mini, micro (PC), embedded. Analog, digital, hybrid computers.
- Hardware: Input devices (keyboard, mouse – Douglas Engelbart, scanner, joystick, light pen, microphone, webcam, OMR, OCR, MICR – cheques, barcode reader, biometric sensor), output devices (monitor – VDU, printer – impact (dot matrix) & non-impact (laser, inkjet), plotter, speaker, projector), CPU (ALU – arithmetic & logic; CU – control unit; registers). Motherboard. SMPS – power. UPS – backup.
- Memory: primary – RAM (volatile; SRAM faster used in cache, DRAM main memory) and ROM (non-volatile; PROM, EPROM – erased by UV, EEPROM – electrically, flash); cache (fastest after registers). Secondary – HDD, SSD, CD (700 MB), DVD (4.7 GB), Blu-ray (25 GB per layer), pen drive. Memory hierarchy (fastest to slowest): registers > cache > RAM > SSD > HDD > optical.
| Unit | Equals |
| Bit | Binary digit (0 or 1) |
| Nibble | 4 bits |
| Byte | 8 bits |
| Kilobyte (KB) | 1,024 bytes |
| Megabyte (MB) | 1,024 KB |
| Gigabyte (GB) | 1,024 MB |
| Terabyte (TB) | 1,024 GB |
| Petabyte (PB) | 1,024 TB |
| Exabyte, Zettabyte, Yottabyte | Next in order |
- Number systems: binary (base 2), octal (8), decimal (10), hexadecimal (16; digits 0–9, A–F). ASCII (7-bit, 128 characters), Unicode (all scripts). 1's & 2's complement.
- Software: system software (operating systems – Windows (Microsoft – Bill Gates & Paul Allen), Linux (Linus Torvalds; open source), macOS, Android (Google; Linux-based), iOS, Unix (Ken Thompson & Dennis Ritchie), MS-DOS (CLI)), utility software (antivirus, compression), application software (MS Office – Word, Excel (spreadsheet – cells, formulas), PowerPoint (slides), Access (database)). Firmware (BIOS). Compiler (whole program at once), interpreter (line by line), assembler (assembly → machine code). Booting: cold boot / warm boot.
- Languages: machine language (binary), assembly (low-level), high-level – FORTRAN (first high-level, 1957; scientific), COBOL (business), BASIC, C (Dennis Ritchie, Bell Labs), C++ (Bjarne Stroustrup), Java (James Gosling, Sun Microsystems; 'write once, run anywhere'), Python (Guido van Rossum), HTML (web pages, Tim Berners-Lee), SQL (databases), JavaScript.
- Shortcut keys (Windows/MS Office): Ctrl+C copy, Ctrl+X cut, Ctrl+V paste, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo, Ctrl+S save, Ctrl+P print, Ctrl+A select all, Ctrl+F find, Ctrl+H replace, Ctrl+B bold, Ctrl+I italic, Ctrl+U underline, Ctrl+N new, Ctrl+O open, Ctrl+W/Ctrl+F4 close document, Alt+F4 close window, F1 help, F2 rename, F5 refresh/slide show, F7 spell check (Word), F12 save as, Ctrl+Alt+Del task manager/security, Windows+D desktop, Windows+L lock, Ctrl+Home – beginning of document, Shift+F3 change case.

## 15.4 Internet, Networks and Cyber Security
- ARPANET (1969, USA) – precursor of the internet. TCP/IP – Vint Cerf & Bob Kahn ('fathers of the internet'). World Wide Web – Tim Berners-Lee (1989, CERN). First web browser – WorldWideWeb/Nexus; Mosaic – first popular graphical browser. Internet in India: VSNL launched public internet on 15 August 1995.
- Networks: LAN (building), MAN (city), WAN (country/world; internet is the largest WAN), PAN (personal, Bluetooth). Topologies: bus, star (most common; central hub/switch), ring, mesh, tree. Devices: hub, switch, router (connects networks), modem (modulator–demodulator; digital ↔ analog), gateway, repeater, bridge, NIC. Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11), Bluetooth (short range, Ericsson), NFC, Li-Fi (light; Harald Haas).
- Protocols: HTTP/HTTPS (web; S = secure, SSL/TLS), FTP (file transfer), SMTP (sending email), POP3/IMAP (receiving email), DNS (domain name → IP address), DHCP (assigns IP), TCP (reliable) and UDP (fast, connectionless), IP address – IPv4 (32-bit), IPv6 (128-bit). URL – Uniform Resource Locator. Domains: .com (commercial), .org, .edu, .gov, .in (India), .net.
- Email: first sent by Ray Tomlinson (1971; chose @). CC (carbon copy), BCC (blind carbon copy). Spam – unsolicited bulk email.
- Cyber threats: virus (attaches to programs; needs host), worm (self-replicating, spreads over network), Trojan horse (disguised as useful software), ransomware (encrypts data and demands ransom – WannaCry 2017), spyware, adware, phishing (fraudulent emails/sites to steal details), vishing (voice), smishing (SMS), keylogger, botnet, DDoS (denial of service), hacking (white hat – ethical; black hat), cookies (small data files stored by websites), firewall (blocks unauthorised access), antivirus, encryption, two-factor authentication, CAPTCHA (distinguishes humans from bots). First computer virus – Creeper (1971); first PC virus – Brain (1986, Pakistan).
- India: IT Act 2000 (cyber law; amended 2008); CERT-In (national cyber security agency, 2004); Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; Aadhaar (UIDAI, 2009; 12 digits; first issued to Ranjana Sonawane, 2010); DigiLocker, UPI (2016), BHIM, CoWIN, ONDC, Bharat Net (rural broadband), 5G launch in India (1 October 2022).
- Emerging tech: AI (term coined by John McCarthy, 1956), machine learning, blockchain (cryptocurrency – Bitcoin by 'Satoshi Nakamoto', 2009), Internet of Things, cloud computing (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS), quantum computing (qubits; National Quantum Mission 2023), 3D printing, augmented & virtual reality, metaverse, Large Language Models (ChatGPT, 2022), semiconductor fabs (India Semiconductor Mission).
F: ARPANET 1969 | WWW – Berners-Lee 1989 | Internet in India – 15 Aug 1995 (VSNL) | IPv4 32-bit, IPv6 128-bit | IT Act 2000 | 5G India – 1 Oct 2022 | Email – Ray Tomlinson 1971 | AI term – John McCarthy 1956
