# Modern Indian History

## 3.1 Arrival of Europeans
| Power | Arrival / company | First factory & key facts |
| Portuguese | Vasco da Gama reached Calicut 1498 (Zamorin) | Francisco de Almeida first viceroy (Blue Water policy); Albuquerque captured Goa from Bijapur (1510); last to leave (Goa liberated 1961) |

| English | East India Company, charter by Queen Elizabeth I, 31 Dec 1600 | Surat factory 1613 (Jahangir's farman); Madras (Fort St. George, 1639–40); Bombay (dowry from Portugal to Charles II, 1661); Calcutta founded by Job Charnock (1690) |
| Danes | 1616 | Tranquebar (Tamil Nadu), Serampore (Bengal) |

F: Vasco da Gama – Calicut 1498 | EIC – 1600 | Surat factory – 1613 | Wandiwash – 1760 | Goa liberated – 1961
PYQ-TREND: First Europeans to come and last to leave (Portuguese). Vasco da Gama's arrival at Calicut. Battle of Wandiwash. Founder of Calcutta. Bombay as dowry.

## 3.2 British Conquest
- Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal) through Mir Jafar's treachery. Black Hole tragedy (1756) preceded it.
- Battle of Buxar (1764): British defeated the combined army of Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh) and Shah Alam II (Mughal). Treaty of Allahabad (1765): Company got Diwani (revenue rights) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa. Clive introduced Dual Government in Bengal (1765–72).
- Anglo-Mysore wars: Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan. 1st (1767–69, Treaty of Madras), 2nd (1780–84, Treaty of Mangalore), 3rd (1790–92, Treaty of Seringapatam – Cornwallis), 4th (1799 – Tipu killed at Srirangapatna; Wellesley). Tipu planted a 'Tree of Liberty' at Srirangapatna and became a member of the Jacobin Club.
- Policies of expansion: Subsidiary Alliance (Lord Wellesley, 1798; first – Nizam of Hyderabad); Doctrine of Lapse (Lord Dalhousie; first – Satara 1848; also Jhansi, Nagpur, Sambalpur, Udaipur); Awadh annexed in 1856 on grounds of misgovernment.
F: Plassey 1757 (Clive v Siraj) | Buxar 1764 | Diwani 1765 (Allahabad) | Subsidiary alliance – Wellesley | Doctrine of Lapse – Dalhousie
PYQ-TREND: Plassey vs Buxar participants and years. Treaty of Allahabad. Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley) and first state to accept it (Hyderabad). Doctrine of Lapse states. Fourth Anglo-Mysore War and Tipu's death.

## 3.5 Revolt of 1857
- Immediate cause: greased cartridges of the Enfield rifle. Mangal Pandey attacked officers at Barrackpore (29 March 1857). Outbreak at Meerut on 10 May 1857; rebels marched to Delhi and proclaimed Bahadur Shah Zafar emperor.
| Centre | Leader | British officer who suppressed |
| Delhi | Bahadur Shah Zafar, General Bakht Khan | John Nicholson, Hodson |
| Kanpur | Nana Saheb, Tantia Tope | Colin Campbell |
| Lucknow | Begum Hazrat Mahal | Colin Campbell |
| Jhansi | Rani Lakshmibai | Hugh Rose |
| Bihar (Jagdishpur) | Kunwar Singh | William Taylor, Vincent Eyre |
| Faizabad | Maulvi Ahmadullah | – |
| Bareilly | Khan Bahadur Khan | – |
- Results: Government of India Act 1858 ended Company rule; Queen Victoria's Proclamation (Allahabad, 1 Nov 1858); post of Secretary of State for India; Viceroy title.
- Books: 'The Indian War of Independence 1857' – V.D. Savarkar; '1857: The Great Rebellion' – Ashok Mehta; S.B. Chaudhuri on civil rebellions.
F: 1857 began at Meerut on 10 May | Mangal Pandey – Barrackpore | GoI Act 1858 ended Company rule
PYQ-TREND: Leaders at each centre of 1857 (especially Lucknow – Begum Hazrat Mahal, Bihar – Kunwar Singh, Jhansi – Lakshmibai, Kanpur – Nana Saheb). Starting place Meerut. Savarkar called it the first war of independence.

## 3.7 Indian National Congress and Early Nationalism
- Precursors: Landholders' Society (1838), British Indian Association (1851), East India Association (Dadabhai Naoroji, London 1866), Indian Association (Surendranath Banerjee & Anand Mohan Bose, 1876), Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870), Madras Mahajan Sabha (1884).
- INC founded on 28 December 1885 at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay, by A.O. Hume; 72 delegates; first president W.C. Bonnerjee.

## 3.8 Gandhian Era (1915–1947)
- Gandhi returned from South Africa on 9 January 1915 (now Pravasi Bharatiya Divas). Political guru Gokhale. Founded Sabarmati Ashram (1917, earlier Kochrab 1915). In South Africa: Natal Indian Congress (1894), Phoenix Farm, Tolstoy Farm; newspaper Indian Opinion.

PYQ-TREND: Champaran as Gandhi's first satyagraha in India. Jallianwala Bagh date and General Dyer. Chauri Chaura and withdrawal of NCM. Dandi March dates. 'Do or Die' and Quit India. Second Round Table – only one Gandhi attended. Poona Pact. First individual satyagrahi. Kakori and Chittagong. Bhagat Singh's execution date. Simon Commission and Lala Lajpat Rai.
TIP: Gandhi's movements in order with a 10–12 year rhythm: NCM 1920 → CDM 1930 → QIM 1942.
