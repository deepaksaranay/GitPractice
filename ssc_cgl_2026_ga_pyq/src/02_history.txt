# History – Reported Questions & Explanations

## 2.1 Founder of the Mauryan Empire
RQ: Who founded the Mauryan Empire? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper]
ANS: Chandragupta Maurya (c. 321 BCE), with the guidance of Chanakya (Kautilya). (verified)
Explanation. Chandragupta overthrew the last Nanda king, Dhanananda, and established his capital at Pataliputra (near modern Patna). He defeated Seleucus Nikator (305 BCE); the treaty gave him Arachosia, Gedrosia and Paropamisadae, and Seleucus sent Megasthenes as ambassador, who wrote 'Indica'. Late in life Chandragupta adopted Jainism under Bhadrabahu and died at Shravanabelagola (Karnataka) by sallekhana. His successors: Bindusara ('Amitraghata') and Ashoka (Kalinga war 261 BCE). The last Mauryan ruler, Brihadratha, was killed by his general Pushyamitra Shunga (185 BCE).
F: Chandragupta Maurya – founder (321 BCE) | Chanakya – Arthashastra | Megasthenes – Indica | Seleucus defeated 305 BCE | Died at Shravanabelagola
PYQ-TREND: Revise founders of dynasties: Haryanka – Bimbisara, Nanda – Mahapadma Nanda, Shunga – Pushyamitra, Kanva – Vasudeva, Satavahana – Simuka, Gupta – Sri Gupta (Chandragupta I real founder), Pushyabhuti – Pushyabhuti (Harsha), Pallava – Simhavishnu, Chalukya (Badami) – Pulakeshin I, Rashtrakuta – Dantidurga, Pala – Gopala, Chola (imperial) – Vijayalaya, Slave dynasty – Qutbuddin Aibak, Vijayanagara – Harihara & Bukka, Bahmani – Hasan Gangu, Mughal – Babur.

## 2.2 The 18 Puranas
RQ: A question on the number of Puranas (18) was reported. [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · Examshortcut]
ANS: There are 18 Mahapuranas. (verified)
Explanation. The Puranas are Sanskrit texts of the post-Vedic period, compiled mostly in the Gupta age, traditionally attributed to Vyasa. They describe creation (sarga), re-creation (pratisarga), genealogy of gods and sages (vamsha), the ages of Manu (manvantara) and dynasties of kings (vamshanucharita) – these five are called 'Pancha Lakshana'. The 18 Mahapuranas include Vishnu, Bhagavata, Shiva, Vayu, Matsya, Markandeya, Brahma, Brahmanda, Padma, Agni, Garuda, Kurma, Linga, Narada, Skanda, Vamana, Varaha and Bhavishya Purana. The Matsya Purana is regarded as the oldest; the Vayu, Vishnu and Matsya Puranas give dynastic lists of the Mauryas, Satavahanas and Guptas – useful historical sources. There are also 18 Upapuranas.
| Number to remember | Text |
| 4 | Vedas |
| 6 | Vedangas; schools of philosophy (Darshanas) |
| 18 | Mahapuranas; chapters (parvas) of the Mahabharata; chapters of the Bhagavad Gita |
| 108 | Upanishads (traditional count) |
| 1028 | Hymns in the Rigveda (10 mandalas) |
| 24,000 | Verses in the Ramayana (7 kandas) |
| 1,00,000 | Verses in the Mahabharata ('Shatasahasri Samhita') |
F: 18 Mahapuranas | Matsya Purana oldest | Pancha Lakshana – 5 topics | Mahabharata – 18 parvas, 1 lakh verses | Ramayana – 7 kandas, 24,000 verses
PYQ-TREND: Number-based questions on ancient texts; Upanishad source of 'Satyameva Jayate' (Mundaka); Samaveda and music; Gita's 18 chapters.

## 2.3 Fatawa-e-Alamgiri
RQ: In which year/whose reign was the Fatawa-e-Alamgiri compiled? [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · Examshortcut]
ANS: Under Aurangzeb (Alamgir), compiled roughly between 1664 and 1672. (verified; exact years vary slightly by source)
Explanation. The Fatawa-e-Alamgiri (also Fatawa-i-Hindiyya) is a compilation of Islamic law based on the Hanafi school, prepared by a board of jurists led by Sheikh Nizamuddin Burhanpuri on Aurangzeb's orders. It became the basis of law in the Mughal Empire and was later used by British courts for Muslim personal law. Other Aurangzeb facts: re-imposed jizya (1679); banned music at court (but he played the veena himself, per tradition); executed Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675); built Moti Masjid in the Red Fort, Delhi, and the Badshahi Mosque, Lahore; Bibi ka Maqbara (Aurangabad) was built by his son Azam Shah for his mother Dilras Banu Begum; 'Zinda Pir'; died at Ahmednagar (1707); buried at Khuldabad.

| Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri | Jahangir |
| Padshahnama | Abdul Hamid Lahori (Shah Jahan) |

PYQ-TREND: Mughal books and authors; Aurangzeb's policies; Dara Shikoh's translations; Bibi ka Maqbara.

## 2.4 Taj Mahal
RQ: A question on the Taj Mahal (timeline and patron) was reported. [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · PW topic list]
ANS: Built by Shah Jahan in memory of Mumtaz Mahal (Arjumand Banu Begum); construction c. 1632–1653; chief architect Ustad Ahmad Lahori; UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1983. (verified)
Explanation. The Taj stands on the right bank of the Yamuna at Agra. It is built of white Makrana marble (Rajasthan) with pietra dura inlay (parchin kari), set in a charbagh garden. It was declared one of the New Seven Wonders of the World (2007). Air pollution (SO₂ from the Mathura refinery and other sources) causes 'marble cancer' – yellowing – and the Taj Trapezium Zone was created to protect it. Other Shah Jahan buildings: Red Fort (Delhi), Jama Masjid (Delhi), Moti Masjid (Agra Fort), Shalimar Bagh (Lahore), Peacock Throne (Takht-e-Taus, taken by Nadir Shah in 1739).
F: Taj Mahal – Shah Jahan, for Mumtaz Mahal | 1632–1653 | Ustad Ahmad Lahori | Makrana marble | UNESCO 1983
PYQ-TREND: Mughal monuments and builders – Humayun's Tomb (Hamida Banu Begum; first garden tomb), Buland Darwaza and Fatehpur Sikri (Akbar), Itmad-ud-Daulah's tomb (Nur Jahan; first use of pietra dura), Akbar's tomb at Sikandra (Jahangir), Red Fort & Jama Masjid (Shah Jahan), Bibi ka Maqbara (Azam Shah).

## 2.5 Abolition of Sati
RQ: A question on the abolition of the practice of Sati was reported. [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · PW topic list]
ANS: Sati was abolished by Bengal Regulation XVII of 1829 under Governor-General Lord William Bentinck, with the support of Raja Ram Mohan Roy. (verified)
Explanation. Ram Mohan Roy campaigned against sati through his writings and petitions (his sister-in-law had been forced into it). The regulation of 4 December 1829 applied first to the Bengal Presidency and was extended to Madras and Bombay in 1830. Orthodox groups (Dharma Sabha of Radhakanta Deb) appealed to the Privy Council, which upheld the ban in 1832. Akbar and the Peshwas had earlier tried to discourage sati. After independence, the Commission of Sati (Prevention) Act, 1987 was passed after the Roop Kanwar case (Deorala, Rajasthan).

| Abolition of Sati | 1829 | William Bentinck (Ram Mohan Roy) |
| Female infanticide prohibition | 1795, 1804 (Bengal Regulations) | – |
| Abolition of slavery | 1843 | Ellenborough |

| Native Marriage Act (civil marriage) | 1872 | Northbrook (Keshab Chandra Sen) |
| Age of Consent Act | 1891 | Lansdowne (Behramji Malabari) |
| Sarda Act (Child Marriage Restraint) | 1929 | Irwin (Harbilas Sarda) |
F: Sati abolished – Regulation XVII, 4 Dec 1829, Bentinck + Ram Mohan Roy | Widow Remarriage – 1856 | Sarda Act – 1929
PYQ-TREND: Social reform laws with Governors-General; Brahmo Samaj (1828); Vidyasagar; Sarda Act age limits (girls 14, boys 18 originally).

## 2.6 Kailasanatha Temple
RQ: Which dynasty built the Kailasanatha temple? [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper – reported, not confirmed by other sources]
ANS: Kailasanatha temple, Kanchipuram – Pallava dynasty, built by Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha), c. 700 CE; completed by his son Mahendravarman III. (verified)
Explanation. It is the oldest structural temple in Kanchipuram and an early example of Dravidian architecture in stone (rather than rock-cut). Do not confuse it with the Kailasa (Kailasanatha) temple, Cave 16, Ellora (Maharashtra), a monolithic rock-cut temple built by the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I (8th century). In the exam, read the place carefully: Kanchipuram → Pallava; Ellora → Rashtrakuta.

## 2.7 Stupa – the Umbrella Structure
RQ: What is the umbrella-like structure on top of a stupa called? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper; the source's own answer pairing was unclear]
ANS: Chhatra (chhatravali for the tiered parasols). (verified; 'Harmika' is the square railed platform on top of the dome, below the chhatra)
Explanation. Parts of a stupa from the bottom: medhi (circular drum/base), anda (hemispherical dome – the cosmic mountain; contains the relics), harmika (square railing on top of the anda – the abode of gods), yashti (central mast), chhatra (umbrella/parasol – symbol of royalty and protection), pradakshina patha (circumambulatory path), vedika (railing), torana (gateway – Sanchi's four toranas, 1st century BCE). Sanchi's Great Stupa was commissioned by Ashoka (3rd century BCE) and enlarged under the Shungas and Satavahanas.
| Term | Meaning |
| Chaitya | Prayer hall (with a stupa at the end) – Karle, Bhaja, Ajanta |
| Vihara | Monastery / residence for monks |
| Stupa | Relic mound |
| Torana | Ornamental gateway |
| Mudra | Hand gesture (e.g., Bhumisparsha – touching earth, Dharmachakra – teaching) |
F: Stupa parts: medhi → anda → harmika → yashti → chhatra | Sanchi – Ashoka, toranas
PYQ-TREND: Parts of stupa; Buddhist sites (Sanchi, Sarnath, Bharhut, Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda); mudras.
