# Static GK – Books, Sports, Awards: Reported Questions & Explanations

## 6.1 'Tomb of Sand' – International Booker Prize 2022
RQ: Who won the 2022 International Booker Prize for 'Tomb of Sand'? [CGL 2026 · 30 Sep · Prepp memory-based paper (Q42)]
ANS: Geetanjali Shree (Hindi novel 'Ret Samadhi'), translated into English by Daisy Rockwell. (verified)
Explanation. It was the first book translated from any Indian language to win the International Booker Prize; the £50,000 prize is shared equally between author and translator. The novel follows an 80-year-old widow who travels to Pakistan to confront her unresolved past from Partition. The International Booker is for fiction translated into English and published in the UK/Ireland; the Booker Prize (since 1969) is for fiction originally written in English. In 2025 Banu Mushtaq won the International Booker for 'Heart Lamp' (Kannada; translated by Deepa Bhasthi) – the first Kannada work to win.

## 6.2 Booker Prize (Reported Theme)
RQ: A question on the Booker Prize was reported. [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · Examshortcut]
ANS: The Booker Prize is a British literary award (since 1969) for the best novel written in English and published in the UK or Ireland; prize money £50,000. (verified)
Explanation. Originally sponsored by Booker McConnell; called the 'Man Booker Prize' 2002–2019 under the Man Group; renamed the Booker Prize in 2019. ## 6.3 'A Suitable Boy'
RQ: Who wrote 'A Suitable Boy'? [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper – reported, not confirmed by other sources]
ANS: Vikram Seth (1993). (verified)
Explanation. One of the longest English-language novels published in one volume (~1,350 pages), set in newly independent India (1951–52) around Mrs Rupa Mehra's search for a husband for her daughter Lata. Vikram Seth's other works: The Golden Gate (novel in verse), An Equal Music, From Heaven Lake (travel), Two Lives. He received the Sahitya Akademi Award (1988, The Golden Gate) and Padma Shri (2007).
F: A Suitable Boy – Vikram Seth (1993)
PYQ-TREND: English novels by Indian authors (see 6.4 table).

## 6.4 'Train to Pakistan'
RQ: Who wrote 'Train to Pakistan'? [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · Examshortcut]
ANS: Khushwant Singh (1956). (verified)
Explanation. The novel is set in the fictional border village of Mano Majra during the Partition of 1947 and shows communal violence through the story of Juggut Singh and Nooran. Padma Bhushan (1974; returned in 1984 after Operation Blue Star), Padma Vibhushan (2007).

## 6.5 'Malgudi Days'
RQ: Who wrote 'Malgudi Days'? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper]
ANS: R.K. Narayan (Rasipuram Krishnaswami Iyer Narayanaswami). (verified)
Explanation. Malgudi is a fictional South Indian town that appears in most of Narayan's works. Malgudi Days (1943) is a collection of short stories; the TV series (1986) was directed by Shankar Nag with sketches by R.K. Laxman (Narayan's younger brother, creator of 'The Common Man'). Other works: Swami and Friends (first novel, 1935), The Guide (Sahitya Akademi Award 1960; film 'Guide' 1965), The English Teacher, The Financial Expert, The Man-Eater of Malgudi. Padma Vibhushan (2000); nominated to the Rajya Sabha (1985).
F: Malgudi Days – R.K. Narayan | The Guide – Sahitya Akademi 1960 | R.K. Laxman – brother, 'Common Man'
PYQ-TREND: R.K. Narayan's works; Sahitya Akademi winners in English.

## 6.6 First Indian Grand Slam Winner
RQ: Who was the first Indian to win a Grand Slam title? [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · Examshortcut]
ANS: Mahesh Bhupathi – 1997 French Open mixed doubles, with Rika Hiraki (Japan). (verified as reported by the source)
Explanation. Bhupathi later completed a career Grand Slam in mixed doubles. With Leander Paes he won the 1999 French Open and Wimbledon men's doubles. Leander Paes – Olympic bronze 1996 (Atlanta), 18 Grand Slam doubles/mixed titles. Sania Mirza – 6 Grand Slam doubles/mixed titles; first Indian woman to win a WTA title (Hyderabad Open 2005). Rohan Bopanna – 2024 Australian Open men's doubles (with Matthew Ebden), oldest man to become world No. 1 in doubles (aged 43). Ramanathan Krishnan and Vijay Amritraj reached Wimbledon singles quarter/semi-finals. Grand Slams: Australian Open (hard, January), French Open/Roland Garros (clay, May–June), Wimbledon (grass, oldest – 1877), US Open (hard, Aug–Sept).
F: First Indian Grand Slam – Mahesh Bhupathi, 1997 French Open mixed doubles | Wimbledon – grass, oldest (1877) | French Open – clay
PYQ-TREND: Indian tennis firsts; Grand Slam surfaces; Davis Cup (men) and Billie Jean King Cup (women).

## 6.7 Marathon Distance
RQ: What is the official length of a marathon? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper; answer given as option (a) 42.195 km]
ANS: 42.195 km (26 miles 385 yards). (verified)
Explanation. The race commemorates the legend of Pheidippides running from Marathon to Athens (490 BCE) to announce the Greek victory over the Persians. The distance was fixed at 42.195 km from the 1908 London Olympics (start at Windsor Castle, finish before the royal box) and standardised by the IAAF in 1921. Half marathon = 21.0975 km. Ultra-marathon = any distance above 42.195 km.

## 6.8 LBW – Cricket Term
RQ: LBW is associated with which sport? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper]
ANS: Cricket – Leg Before Wicket. (verified)
Explanation. A batter is out LBW if the ball would have hit the stumps but is intercepted by the batter's body (usually the pad), subject to conditions: the ball must not pitch outside leg stump, and if the batter offers a shot, the impact must be in line with the stumps. Decisions can be reviewed through DRS (Decision Review System) using ball-tracking (Hawk-Eye). Cricket laws are maintained by the MCC (Marylebone Cricket Club, Lord's). Modes of dismissal: bowled, caught, LBW, run out, stumped, hit wicket, obstructing the field (includes handling the ball since 2017), hit the ball twice, timed out, and retired out.

## 6.10 Manu Bhaker
RQ: A question on Manu Bhaker was reported. [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · Examshortcut]
ANS: Manu Bhaker (shooter, Haryana) became the first Indian athlete since independence to win two medals at a single Olympics – bronze in women's 10 m air pistol and bronze in 10 m air pistol mixed team (with Sarabjot Singh), Paris 2024. (verified)
Explanation. She also won the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna (announced January 2025). Her bronze in 10 m air pistol was India's first medal at Paris 2024 and India's first Olympic shooting medal by a woman. India's Paris 2024 tally: 6 medals – 1 silver (Neeraj Chopra, javelin) and 5 bronze (Manu Bhaker – 10 m air pistol; Manu Bhaker & Sarabjot Singh – mixed team; Swapnil Kusale – 50 m rifle 3 positions; men's hockey team; Aman Sehrawat – wrestling 57 kg).
F: Manu Bhaker – 2 bronze, Paris 2024 (10 m air pistol; mixed team with Sarabjot Singh) | First Indian with 2 medals in one Games post-independence
PYQ-TREND: Indian Olympic medallists (Bindra 2008 first individual gold; Neeraj Chopra gold 2021; Karnam Malleswari first woman medallist 2000).
