# Art & Culture – Reported Questions & Explanations

## 3.1 Bihu – Assam
RQ: Bihu is the main festival/folk dance of which state? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper]
ANS: Assam. (verified)
Explanation. Assam celebrates three Bihus tied to the agricultural cycle: Bohag/Rongali Bihu (mid-April; Assamese New Year; sowing; dances and songs with dhol, pepa (buffalo-horn pipe), gogona), Kati/Kongali Bihu (mid-October; lean period; lamps lit in paddy fields) and Magh/Bhogali Bihu (mid-January; harvest; feasting; Meji bonfires). Bihu dance holds the Guinness record for the largest Bihu performance (2023, Guwahati, over 11,000 performers). Other Assam forms: Sattriya (classical – Shankardev), Bagurumba (Bodo 'butterfly dance'), Ojapali, Ankia Naat (one-act plays of Shankardev), Jhumur (tea tribes).
F: Bihu – Assam (Rongali April, Kati Oct, Bhogali Jan) | Sattriya – Assam classical | Bagurumba – Bodo

## 3.2 Chapchar Kut and Cheraw – Mizoram
RQ: Chapchar Kut is celebrated in which state? [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · Examshortcut, PW] and Cheraw dance belongs to which state? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper]
ANS: Both are from Mizoram. (verified)
Explanation. Chapchar Kut is Mizoram's spring festival, held in March after the jhum (shifting cultivation) forest clearing is completed and the cut bamboo is left to dry. It features Cheraw (bamboo dance) and other dances such as Khuallam and Chheih Lam. Cheraw: men sit facing each other and clap long bamboo poles open and shut in rhythm, while women in colourful puan dresses dance between and across them. Other Mizo festivals: Mim Kut (after the maize harvest, honouring the dead) and Pawl Kut (after the paddy harvest; thanksgiving). 'Kut' means festival.
F: Chapchar Kut – Mizoram (spring, after jhum clearing) | Cheraw – bamboo dance, Mizoram | Mim Kut, Pawl Kut – Mizoram
PYQ-TREND: Festival–state pairs from the North-East appear in almost every shift.

## 3.3 Baul – Bengal
RQ: The Baul tradition is associated with which region? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper]
ANS: Bengal (West Bengal and Bangladesh). (verified)
Explanation. Bauls are wandering mystic minstrels whose songs combine elements of Vaishnava bhakti and Sufism, rejecting caste and ritual and seeking the 'Moner Manush' (the person of the heart) within. Instruments: ektara (one-string), dotara, khamak, dubki. Lalon Fakir (Lalon Shah) is the most famous Baul poet. Rabindranath Tagore was influenced by Baul songs. UNESCO inscribed Baul songs on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008 (proclaimed 2005). Joydev Kenduli Mela (Birbhum) is a major Baul gathering.
F: Baul – Bengal mystic minstrels | Lalon Fakir | Ektara | UNESCO ICH (2005/2008)
PYQ-TREND: Folk music forms by state – Pandavani (Chhattisgarh – Teejan Bai), Lavani (Maharashtra), Bhatiali (Bengal boatmen), Bihugeet (Assam), Mando (Goa), Maand (Rajasthan), Kajri & Chaiti (UP–Bihar), Sohar (Bihar), Powada (Maharashtra – ballads), Naatupura Paatu (Tamil Nadu), Rabindra Sangeet & Nazrul Geeti (Bengal).

## 3.4 Folk Dances of Odisha
RQ: A question on folk dances of Odisha was reported. [CGL 2026 · 1 Oct · Shift 1 · PW topic list]
ANS: Major folk dances of Odisha: Chhau (Mayurbhanj style – no masks), Ghumura, Dalkhai, Gotipua, Sambalpuri, Paika (martial), Ranappa (stilts), Karma, Danda Nata. Classical: Odissi. (verified)
Explanation. Gotipua – boys dressed as girls dance in praise of Jagannath; it is the predecessor of Odissi, along with the Mahari (devadasi) tradition. Ghumura – dance with a drum (ghumura) tied to the chest, from Kalahandi. Dalkhai – Sambalpur region, performed by women during Dussehra, sung in praise of the goddess. Chhau – three styles: Purulia (West Bengal) and Seraikela (Jharkhand) use masks; Mayurbhanj (Odisha) does not; inscribed on UNESCO's ICH list in 2010. Paika – martial dance of the Paikas (militia; Paika rebellion 1817). Odissi: tribhanga posture; dancers Kelucharan Mohapatra, Sonal Mansingh, Protima Bedi (Nrityagram).
F: Odisha folk – Gotipua, Ghumura, Dalkhai, Chhau (Mayurbhanj, no mask), Paika, Ranappa | Odissi – classical | Chhau UNESCO 2010
PYQ-TREND: Revise folk dances state-wise (table in GS notes Chapter 4); masks in Chhau (Purulia & Seraikela yes, Mayurbhanj no).

## 3.5 Classical Dances (Reported Theme)
RQ: Classical dances were reported among the GA topics. [CGL 2026 · 30 Sep · Shift 1 · PW]
ANS: Sangeet Natak Akademi recognises 8 classical dances: Bharatanatyam (Tamil Nadu), Kathak (UP), Kathakali (Kerala), Mohiniyattam (Kerala), Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh), Odissi (Odisha), Manipuri (Manipur), Sattriya (Assam). The Ministry of Culture also includes Chhau. (verified)
Explanation. Questions usually ask the state of a dance, its features or a famous exponent. Key features: Bharatanatyam – solo, fixed upper torso, Natyashastra-based, earlier 'Sadir'; Kathak – chakkars (spins), Mughal court influence, gharanas of Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras, Raigarh; Kathakali – dance-drama with heavy make-up (pacha = green for noble characters, kathi, thadi, kari), male performers, chenda drum; Mohiniyattam – lasya, solo female, white and gold costume; Kuchipudi – Tarangam (dancing on the rim of a brass plate with a pot of water on the head); Odissi – tribhanga, chauka; Manipuri – Ras Lila, gentle movements, no ankle bells; Sattriya – Shankardev, sattras (monasteries), Ankia Naat.

## 3.6 Bismillah Khan – Shehnai
RQ: Bismillah Khan is associated with which instrument? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper]
ANS: Shehnai. (verified)
Explanation. Ustad Bismillah Khan (1916–2006) of Varanasi (born in Dumraon, Bihar) took the shehnai from weddings and temples to the concert stage. He played the shehnai at the Red Fort on 15 August 1947 and on the first Republic Day (1950). Bharat Ratna 2001 – the third classical musician after M.S. Subbulakshmi (1998) and Ravi Shankar (1999). The shehnai is a double-reed wind (sushira) instrument.
F: Bismillah Khan – shehnai, Varanasi, Bharat Ratna 2001 | Played on 15 Aug 1947 at Red Fort

## 3.7 Hariprasad Chaurasia – Flute
RQ: Pandit Hariprasad Chaurasia is famous for which instrument? [CGL 2026 · 5 Oct · Prepp memory-based paper]
ANS: Bansuri (flute). (verified)
Explanation. Hariprasad Chaurasia (born 1938, Prayagraj) is a Hindustani classical flautist, disciple of Annapurna Devi. He partnered santoor player Shivkumar Sharma as 'Shiv–Hari' for Hindi film music (Silsila, Chandni, Lamhe). Awards: Padma Bhushan (1992), Padma Vibhushan (2000). He founded the Vrindaban Gurukul (Mumbai, Bhubaneswar). Pannalal Ghosh popularised the bansuri as a concert instrument earlier.
