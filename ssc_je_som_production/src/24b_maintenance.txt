# Maintenance, Safety, Value Engineering and Costing

## 24B.1 Maintenance
| Type | Description |
| Breakdown (corrective) maintenance | Repair after failure – suitable for cheap, non-critical items |
| Preventive (time-based) maintenance | Scheduled inspection, lubrication, replacement at fixed intervals – reduces breakdowns |
| Predictive (condition-based) maintenance | Monitoring condition – vibration analysis, oil analysis, thermography, ultrasonic, motor current – repair when indicators show deterioration |
| Proactive / reliability-centred maintenance (RCM) | Analyses failure modes and selects best maintenance for each function |
| Total productive maintenance (TPM) | Operators involved in routine maintenance; aims at zero breakdowns, zero defects; overall equipment effectiveness (OEE) |
| Opportunistic, shutdown, design-out maintenance | Work during planned stops; redesign to eliminate recurring failure |
F: OEE = availability × performance rate × quality rate (world class ≈ 85%)
F: Availability = MTBF/(MTBF + MTTR)
- Replacement analysis: items that deteriorate (machines) are replaced when the average annual cost (capital + running) is minimum; items that fail suddenly (bulbs) – group vs individual replacement.
- Depreciation methods: straight-line (D = (P − S)/n), declining balance (fixed percentage of book value), sum-of-years' digits, sinking fund.
PYQ-TREND: Preventive vs predictive maintenance (frequent); vibration analysis for condition monitoring; TPM and OEE; straight-line depreciation formula; replacement when average annual cost is minimum.

## 24B.2 Industrial Safety
- Causes of accidents: unsafe acts (about 85–88% per Heinrich – carelessness, not using guards or PPE, improper lifting) and unsafe conditions (unguarded machines, poor lighting, slippery floors, defective tools). Heinrich's ratio 1 : 29 : 300 (major injury : minor injuries : no-injury accidents).
F: Frequency rate = (number of lost-time injuries × 10⁶)/man-hours worked
F: Severity rate = (man-days lost × 10⁶)/man-hours worked (in India per IS 3786; some texts use 1000 man-hours)
- Personal protective equipment (PPE): helmets, safety goggles and face shields (welding filters), ear plugs/muffs, gloves, safety shoes, aprons, respirators, safety belts/harness.
- Machine guarding: fixed, interlocked, automatic, adjustable guards; emergency stops; lock-out/tag-out during maintenance.
| Fire class | Fuel | Extinguisher |
| A | Ordinary solids – wood, paper, cloth | Water, foam, ABC powder |
| B | Flammable liquids – oil, petrol | Foam, CO₂, dry chemical powder (never water jet) |
| C | Flammable gases | Dry chemical powder; isolate supply |
| D | Metals – Mg, Na, K | Special dry powder (graphite, sand) |
| Electrical (E in some standards) | Live electrical equipment | CO₂, dry chemical powder (non-conducting) |
| F/K | Cooking oils/fats | Wet chemical |
- Colour codes (IS 9457 typical): red – stop/fire/danger; yellow – caution; green – safety/first aid; blue – mandatory instruction.
- Statutes in India: Factories Act 1948 (now consolidated in the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020), Indian Boilers Act 1923 (a new Boilers Act was passed in 2025), Workmen's Compensation Act.
PYQ-TREND: Fire classes and extinguishers (water not for oil and electrical fires) (very frequent); unsafe acts cause most accidents; Heinrich's ratio; frequency and severity rates; PPE for welding; Factories Act 1948; colour codes.

## 24B.3 Value Engineering, Costing and Ergonomics
- Value = function/cost (or worth/cost). Value engineering/analysis (L. D. Miles, General Electric) systematically identifies unnecessary cost without reducing function. Phases: information, function analysis, creative (brainstorming), evaluation, development/recommendation, implementation. Types of value: use, esteem, cost, exchange.
| Cost element | Meaning |
| Prime cost | Direct material + direct labour + direct expenses |
| Factory (works) cost | Prime cost + factory overheads |
| Cost of production | Factory cost + administrative overheads |
| Total cost (cost of sales) | Cost of production + selling and distribution overheads |
| Selling price | Total cost + profit |
- Fixed costs (rent, depreciation, salaries) do not vary with output; variable costs (material, direct labour) vary proportionally. Contribution = selling price − variable cost per unit. Profit–volume ratio = contribution/sales.
- Overhead allocation methods: percentage of direct labour cost, direct material cost, prime cost, labour-hour rate, machine-hour rate (most accurate for machine-intensive shops).
- Ergonomics: fitting the job to the worker – anthropometric data (5th to 95th percentile design), workplace and display/control design, environmental factors (illumination ≈ 300–500 lux for general work, noise limit 90 dB(A) for 8 h in India, thermal comfort), reduction of fatigue.
- Wage incentive plans: Halsey (50% of time saved paid), Rowan (bonus = time saved/time allowed × time taken × rate), Taylor's differential piece rate, Gantt task and bonus, Emerson efficiency plan, Bedaux point system.
F: Halsey bonus = (time saved × rate)/2;  Rowan bonus = (time saved/time allowed) × time taken × rate
PYQ-TREND: Value = function/cost (very frequent); prime cost = direct material + labour + expenses; machine-hour rate; Halsey and Rowan plans; fixed vs variable cost; noise limit 90 dB; anthropometry 5th–95th percentile.
TIP: Cost ladder: Prime → +Factory OH → Works cost → +Admin OH → Cost of production → +Selling OH → Total cost → +Profit → Selling price.
