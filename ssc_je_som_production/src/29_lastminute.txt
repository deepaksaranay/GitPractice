## 29.3 Comparison Quick-Hits
| Topic | Remember |
| Ductility vs malleability | Tension (wires) vs compression (sheets) |
| Tresca vs von Mises | Hexagon, conservative vs ellipse, accurate |
| Izod vs Charpy | Cantilever vs simply supported |
| Annealing vs normalising | Furnace cool (softer) vs air cool (finer, stronger) |
| Hot vs cold chamber die casting | Zn/Pb/Sn vs Al/brass |
| Misrun vs cold shut | Not filled vs not fused |
| DCSP vs DCRP | Deep penetration vs thin sheets/Al cleaning |
| TIG vs MIG | Non-consumable W vs consumable wire |
| Blanking vs punching | Piece is product vs hole is product |
| Direct vs indirect extrusion | More vs less friction |
| Up vs down milling | Lifts work, backlash-safe vs presses work, better finish |
| Hobbing vs gear shaping | Fastest, no internal gears vs internal gears possible |
| EDM vs ECM | Sparks, tool wears vs electrolysis, no tool wear |
| Jig vs fixture | Guides tool vs only holds |
| PERT vs CPM | Probabilistic, event vs deterministic, activity |
| Product vs process layout | Mass vs job/batch production |

## 29.4 Traps Toppers Avoid
- Use effective length (not actual length) and least radius of gyration in column formulas.
- In thin cylinders, hoop stress uses longitudinal joint efficiency and longitudinal stress uses circumferential joint efficiency.
- Maximum shear stress in a triangle section is at mid-height, not at the neutral axis.
- Springs in series: reciprocals of stiffness add (softer combination).
- Shake allowance is negative; all other pattern allowances are positive.
- Normalised steel is harder than annealed steel – not softer.
- Clearance goes on the punch in blanking and on the die in punching.
- Chip thickness ratio is less than 1; the chip is thicker than the uncut layer.
- Lead screw is used only for thread cutting; feed rod for ordinary turning.
- Hard materials need soft-grade grinding wheels.
- GO gauge gets wear allowance; NO-GO gauge does not.
- EOQ varies as square root of demand – doubling demand raises EOQ by 41%, not 100%.

- Processes: casting allowances and defects, special casting, welding processes and polarity, forming calculations, press work.
- Machining: Merchant, Taylor, tool materials, lathe/milling/drilling facts, grinding wheel marking, NTM processes.
- Metrology and IE: fits and tolerances, gauges, instruments, CNC codes, jigs, work study, EOQ, control charts, PERT/CPM.
