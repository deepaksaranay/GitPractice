# Entropy

## 5.1 Clausius Inequality and Entropy
F: Clausius inequality: ∮ δQ/T ≤ 0  (= 0 for reversible cycles, < 0 for irreversible cycles)
F: Definition: dS = (δQ/T)_rev;  S₂ − S₁ = ∫(δQ/T)_rev  (kJ/K; specific s in kJ/kg·K)
F: For any process: dS ≥ δQ/T;  S₂ − S₁ = ∫δQ/T + S_gen (S_gen ≥ 0)
- Entropy is a property (∮dS = 0 for a reversible cycle), a measure of molecular disorder / randomness and of the unavailability of energy.
- On a T–s diagram the area under a reversible process curve equals the heat transfer.
- Entropy can be transferred by heat (and by mass in open systems) – never by work. It is generated by irreversibilities.
PYQ-TREND: Clausius inequality; dS = δQ_rev/T; entropy as a property; area under T–s = heat; work does not transfer entropy.

## 5.2 Principle of Increase of Entropy
F: Isolated system: ΔS_isolated ≥ 0;  ΔS_universe = ΔS_system + ΔS_surroundings ≥ 0
- Entropy of an isolated system (or the universe) never decreases. ΔS > 0 for real (irreversible) processes, = 0 for reversible ones, < 0 impossible.
- Entropy of a system can decrease (e.g., when it is cooled) provided the surroundings' entropy increases by more.
F: Heat transfer Q from T_H to T_L: ΔS_gen = Q(1/T_L − 1/T_H) > 0
PYQ-TREND: Entropy of isolated system never decreases (very frequent); entropy generated in heat transfer across finite ΔT; entropy of a system may decrease.

## 5.3 Entropy Change of Ideal Gases, Solids and Liquids
F: Tds relations: T ds = du + p dv;  T ds = dh − v dp
F: Ideal gas: s₂ − s₁ = cv ln(T₂/T₁) + R ln(v₂/v₁) = cp ln(T₂/T₁) − R ln(p₂/p₁) = cv ln(p₂/p₁) + cp ln(v₂/v₁)
F: Isothermal: Δs = R ln(v₂/v₁) = R ln(p₁/p₂)
F: Isochoric: Δs = cv ln(T₂/T₁);  Isobaric: Δs = cp ln(T₂/T₁)
F: Polytropic: Δs = cv (n − γ)/(n − 1) ln(T₂/T₁)
F: Reversible adiabatic: Δs = 0 (isentropic)
F: Solids/liquids: Δs = c ln(T₂/T₁)
F: Phase change at constant T: Δs = h_fg/T_sat (water at 100 °C: 2257/373.15 = 6.05 kJ/kg·K)
F: Mixing of two equal masses at T₁ and T₂: Δs = 2mc ln[(T₁ + T₂)/(2√(T₁T₂))] > 0 (AM > GM)
- On a T–s diagram, constant-volume lines are steeper than constant-pressure lines (slope T/cv > T/cp).
- On an h–s (Mollier) diagram for an ideal gas, isobars diverge to the right.
- Free expansion of ideal gas into vacuum (volume doubles): Δs = R ln 2 > 0 although Q = 0 – because the process is irreversible.
PYQ-TREND: Entropy change formulas for each process; reversible adiabatic = isentropic (very frequent); isochore steeper than isobar on T–s; entropy change in free expansion; entropy change of vaporisation.
WARN: Adiabatic does not always mean isentropic. An irreversible adiabatic process (throttling, adiabatic friction) has Δs > 0. Only reversible adiabatic = isentropic.

## 5.4 Third Law and Absolute Entropy
- Third law (Nernst heat theorem / Planck statement): the entropy of a pure, perfectly crystalline substance at absolute zero is zero. Consequence: absolute zero cannot be reached in a finite number of steps.
- Allows absolute entropies to be tabulated (useful in chemical thermodynamics).
PYQ-TREND: Third law statement; unattainability of absolute zero.

## 5.5 Isentropic Efficiencies
F: Turbine: η_T = actual work/isentropic work = (h₁ − h₂)/(h₁ − h₂s)
F: Compressor/pump: η_C = isentropic work/actual work = (h₂s − h₁)/(h₂ − h₁)
F: Nozzle: η_N = actual KE at exit / isentropic KE = (h₁ − h₂)/(h₁ − h₂s)
- Irreversibilities make the actual exit state lie to the right of the isentropic state on h–s/T–s diagrams (higher entropy).
PYQ-TREND: Definitions of isentropic efficiency of turbine and compressor (note the inverse ratio for compressors).
Q: State the principle of increase of entropy and prove that entropy of an isolated system increases in any irreversible process.
For any process, the Clausius inequality gives dS ≥ δQ/T, with equality for reversible processes. An isolated system exchanges no heat (δQ = 0), so dS_isolated ≥ 0: its entropy either increases (irreversible process) or remains constant (reversible process); it can never decrease. Since the universe (system + surroundings) is an isolated system, ΔS_universe = ΔS_sys + ΔS_surr ≥ 0. Example: heat Q flowing from a hot body at T_H to a cold body at T_L: ΔS = −Q/T_H + Q/T_L = Q(T_H − T_L)/(T_H T_L) > 0 since T_H > T_L. The increase measures the irreversibility and the degradation of energy; this principle is why natural processes have a direction.
